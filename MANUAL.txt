 CONSOLE COLLEGE 26 · THE MANUAL

  An immersion-first college football simulation. You can coach a program, run one as its
  athletic director, or sit back and watch a whole sport live and breathe for a hundred years.

  This manual covers everything, start to finish. You can read it here, or in the game:
  press [?] on the dashboard (or tab 8 SYSTEM -> [H]). In the game you can jump to any
  chapter, page through it, and search every word.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 1 · QUICK START
================================================================================================

1.1  Run the game
-----------------
  Terminal:     python main.py          (Python 3.8 or newer, no extra packages)
  Window:       double-click play.bat (Windows) or play.command (Mac/Linux),
                or run: python play.py
  Browser:      python play.py --browser

  The first time you run play.py, it offers to install one small package (pywebview) that
  gives the game its own window. Say no, and it opens in your web browser instead. It's the
  same game and the same saves in all three.

1.2  Your first ten minutes (Coach Career)
------------------------------------------
  1. Choose [3] New World. The world takes about half a minute to build: four years of
     recruiting classes, transfers and player development run before you arrive, so the
     rosters you inherit were built the same way every future roster will be.
  2. Choose [1] Coach Career. Name your coach, pick an age and one of six backgrounds, and
     pick your offense, defense, fourth-down style and blitz rate, then a difficulty
     (Freshman, Varsity, All-American, Golden Helmet) and how big a name you are (Unknown, Rising,
     Established, Elite). Enter takes the suggested choice at every step.
  3. Four programs offer you a job: small schools for an Unknown, blue bloods for an Elite
     name. Read the goals, the AD's style and the
     contract. Take one with its number, or try to negotiate ([N#]) — careful, the AD can
     walk away.
  4. You land on the dashboard. Press [4] then [R] for the recruiting hub, [G] for players
     the staff suggests, and add a dozen to your board (A1,2,3...). Recruiting hours start
     in Week 1; the preseason is for building the board.
  5. Press [A]. Fall camp opens: your schedule, your projected wins, your depth chart (set by
     your staff), your inbox. Press Enter to kick off Week 1.
  6. Each week: the week hub shows film, mail, practice focus and an optional press
     conference. A routine ([1]-[5], or your own) sets the practice focus, game plan and
     openers in one key; make one your default and the hub opens with it. Enter goes to
     Saturday. Pick how to play your game — sim it, play the big moments, coach every snap,
     or watch the broadcast. Afterward, THE WEEK'S WORK shows what each decision was worth.
  7. Between games, spend your recruiting hours, or set up standing orders
     (recruiting hub -> [Q]) so the calls and visits happen every week without you.

  Everything else in this manual is depth. You can play a whole career knowing only this.

1.3  The three rules worth knowing on day one
---------------------------------------------
  * Your AD judges you against what your roster should win, not against a perfect season.
    Beat expectations and hit his goals inside their windows.
  * Recruits care about three specific things. Find out what they are (evaluate film, or
    pitch and see how they react) and sell those things.
  * In Coach Career you never see real ratings. You see what a head coach sees: words,
    practice, film. That is on purpose.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 2 · STARTING A WORLD AND CHOOSING HOW TO PLAY
================================================================================================

2.1  The main menu
------------------
  The title screen every time you start:
    [1] Continue     your most recent save (who, where, and when you saved it)
    [2] New Game     pick how to play (Coach Career, Spectator, Athletic Director) — then the
                     world is built (about half a minute) and you're on your way
    [3] Load Game    any save ([X#] deletes one)
    [4] Settings     [5] Manual     [6] About     [Q] Quit
  Enter on the main menu continues your latest save. Picking a mode happens before the world
  is built, so backing out ([B]) costs nothing.
  PLAYER DATABASE IMPORT. Before a new world is built, [P] lets you choose a JSON player database.
  The file does not need one rigid schema. It can be a flat list, {"players": [...]},
  {"teams": {"Alabama": [...]}}, or even {"Alabama": [...], "Georgia": [...]}. A player can provide
  name/player, team/school, year/class, position/pos, traits, and ratings. Ratings can be nested under
  "ratings" or written directly. Missing fields inherit from the generated roster slot being replaced,
  so small modular files are fine. Imported players REPLACE generated players on the matching team; they
  do not make rosters larger. Trait labels shown in game ("Clutch", "Film Junkie") work as well as keys.
  Position is recommended; if omitted, the player inherits the position of the generated slot he replaces.


2.2  The world
--------------
  * 138 FBS programs in ten conferences, with their own stadiums, chants and head coaches, built
    on real states, towns and stadium sizes, and about 70 FCS programs that play guarantee games.
    That's the built-in universe; Settings -> [U] Universe can load another (chapter 33).
  * Every new world is random (the seed shows on tab 8 SYSTEM). It starts in 2022; four
    years of recruiting, the portal and development run with no games, so 2026 rosters have
    history in them but no invented results.
  * The record book you inherit is real: national champions from 1998 through 2025. Every
    title won in your world is added to it.

2.3  The three ways to play
---------------------------
  [1] COACH CAREER — you are a head coach. You recruit, set the depth chart, call plays if
      you want to, answer your mail, face the press, and try not to get fired. Win, and bigger
      jobs call. You never see real ratings.
  [2] SPECTATOR — you control nothing. Every board and coaching change runs itself. Follow
      any team ([F] on the Team tab) and watch any game. All numbers are visible.
  [3] ATHLETIC DIRECTOR — you run a program from the top: hire and fire the coach, write his
      goals and contract, spend on buildings, price tickets and manage what the fans expect.
      The board grades you. See Chapter 22.

2.4  Commissioner Mode
---------------------
  New Game -> [4]. Run a Discord league: members coach programs and send orders; you import them
  and advance the world from the Commissioner Desk. (Hot Seat and its network mode were removed in
  v51; an online mode where everyone runs their own copy of one shared world is being built.)

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 3 · CREATING YOUR COACH AND TAKING YOUR FIRST JOB
================================================================================================

3.1  Name, age and background
-----------------------------
  Age runs 30 to 60 (older coaches start more polished; younger ones have more years).
  Each of the six backgrounds shapes your ratings and gives you a signature trait:

    Offensive Coordinator    quarterbacks love you (passing and offense development)
    Defensive Coordinator    defenses that travel (DB and trench development)
    Recruiting Coordinator   you can win any living room (a big recruiting rating)
    Offensive Line Coach     your lines move people (trench development)
    High School Legend       players run through walls for you (a bit of everything)
    Former Pro League Quarterback   everybody knows your name (passing and recruiting, weak trenches)

3.2  Your schemes
-----------------
  Offense: Air Raid, Spread RPO, Pro Style, West Coast, Smashmouth, Triple Option,
  Power Spread, Veer & Shoot. Defense: 4-3 Zone, 4-2-5 Quarters, Pressure 3-4,
  3-3-5 Stack, Multiple Man, Tite Front.

  Every scheme screen explains what the scheme does, what players it needs, the kind of program it
  resembles, how often it runs and how fast it plays — and which recruits it's an easier or
  harder sell to. Your scheme matters three ways:
    * on Saturday (the plays you call and how often you run),
    * in recruiting (players want to play in schemes that feature their position),
    * in your depth chart (a Smashmouth offense starts two tight ends).
  Your background's suggested schemes are marked, but any combination can win.

3.3  Fourth down and blitzing
-----------------------------
  Fourth down: Conservative (punt, trust the defense), Balanced (go when the numbers say so),
  Aggressive (fourth-and-4 is a green light). Blitz: sit in coverage, pick your spots, or
  bring it. This is what your staff does when they have the headset; you can overrule any
  call when you're coaching.

3.4  The job offers
-------------------
  Four programs offer you a job (or [D] picks any program as a sandbox "dream job"). Who
  calls depends on your prestige (3.6): near the bottom of the sport for an Unknown, the
  blue bloods for an Elite name. Every offer card shows:
    * the program, its conference, prestige and roster strength,
    * the AD's style and how he watches you (how loud one Saturday is, how long the leash),
    * three program goals and how often each is asked for ("once in 4 yrs" vs "every year"),
    * the full contract: years, salary, raises, buyout, offset, release clause, bonuses,
      rollover, the football budget, and what's left for player NIL.

  [#] takes a job. [N#] negotiates: more money, more years, a bigger buyout guarantee, a
  smaller release clause, no offset, or less base for bigger bonuses. Every ask risks the AD
  walking away, the risk climbs with every counter, and whatever you squeeze out of him your
  seat starts that much warmer. See Chapter 16.

  PURSUE A JOB (Coaches menu → [8], any time once you're coaching): your agent calls up to three
  jobs a winter, open ones or seats that could open. Each shows your standing, where you'd land
  on that AD's list: their first call, on the shortlist, in the conversation, a long shot, or not
  on their list. When the carousel reaches a job you pursued and it opened, the AD decides: the
  higher you'd rank, the likelier the offer. An offer comes with your other calls ("you went after
  this one") and the job waits for your answer; a pass tells you why.

3.5  Difficulty
---------------
  Chosen right after your schemes; change it any time (Settings [D], or [L] on your career
  page). It rides with your coach from job to job, and only your program feels it:

                 Game day   Hot seat          Recruiting   The other sideline
  Freshman       +2.5       heats 35% slower  +12%         misreads you more often
  Varsity         0         as tuned          as tuned     as tuned
  All-American   -1.25      heats 15% faster  -7%          reads the film better
  Golden Helmet        -2.5       heats 30% faster  -14%         always has your number

  Game day is rating points of form every Saturday. Your AD still judges you against what
  your roster should win, so the easy levels also cool your seat and the hard ones heat it.
  Against evenly matched teams (400 games each): Freshman won 70%, Varsity 48%,
  All-American 45%, Golden Helmet 38%. Changes are noted on your career page.

3.6  Prestige: how big a name you are
-------------------------------------
  Chosen right after difficulty, once. It's your reputation, not your ability: your ratings
  come from your background whatever you pick. It decides who calls with your first job and
  what they pay. Ranks are FBS programs by prestige (No. 1 is the most prestigious).

                 Who calls                                   First contract
  Unknown        the bottom 45 (No. 94 and below)            first-timer money (the classic start)
  Rising         No. 55-105: top G5, bottom of the Power 4   a little more, a slightly higher cap
  Established    No. 25-70: solid Power 4 programs           a real Power 4 deal, a higher cap
  Elite          the top 30: blue bloods and contenders      no first-timer cap at all

  A bigger name also gets more leverage at the table: the AD sizes you up by your reputation
  when you negotiate, so asks are a little likelier to land. Pay still scales with your
  ratings against the size of the job. After your first season, your record does the talking.

  The catch: an Elite coach with first-year ratings inherits a blue blood's roster, AD and
  goals, and nobody there grades on a curve. Unknown is the long climb; Elite is the deep end.

 CHAPTER 4 · THE DASHBOARD, KEYS, SAVING AND SETTINGS
================================================================================================

4.1  The dashboard
------------------
  The main menu is a dashboard: a scoreboard header for your team (record, conference
  record, poll and NP rank, the date, your inbox count), a tab strip, and live panels.
  Type a number to switch tabs:

    1 HOME         next game, THIS WEEK (what's waiting on you, with the keys to get there),
                   coach, polls, standings, recruiting, headlines
    2 SCHEDULE     [S] your schedule and results  [W] scores around the country
                   [T] conference standings  [O] strength of schedule  [G] another team's schedule
    3 TEAM         [R] roster  [H] depth chart  [P] practice report  [T] team page
                   [$] budget and NIL  [O] look at another team
    4 RECRUITING   [R] recruiting hub  [F] finder  [B] board  [K] class  [P] portal
                   [N] recruiting news
    5 RANKINGS     [T] Media Top 25  [P] NP rankings  [C] selection committee
                   [H] Golden Helmet race  [N] national champions  [S] toughest places to play
                   [O] strength of schedule
    6 COACH        [I] inbox  [C] my career  [T] coaching tree  [D] develop your coach
                   [S] my staff  [K] coaches and carousel  [P] head coach profile
    7 MEDIA        [M] media center  [N] recruiting news  [K] carousel news
    8 SYSTEM       [V] save  [L] load  [E] export to sheets  [S] settings  [H] this manual  [Q] leave

  From any tab: [A] plays the next week (or opens fall camp, or advances to the offseason),
  [M] simulates weeks or seasons, [I] opens your inbox, [C] your career (or the AD office),
  [V] saves, [?] opens this manual, [Q] leaves: back to the main menu or quit (both save first).

  Every screen works the same way: the keys in [brackets] are what you can do, the one in
  green is the main thing to do next, red keys can't be taken back, and [B] (or Enter, where
  the prompt says so) goes back.

4.2  The team page (tab 3 -> [T])
---------------------------------
  Conference, stadium, capacity and crowds, home field (toughest-places rank, noise, home
  record), record, strength of schedule, locker room, Pro League draft picks, coaches and play-callers, budget, prestige,
  team ratings, facilities, program ratings, titles and history. Keys: [R] roster,
  [H] depth chart (yours), [P] a player, [S] schedule, [C] the coach's program profile,
  [$] budget, [F] facilities, [V] rivalries, [K] locker room, [I] instant classics,
  [O]/[D] the coordinators.

4.3  Saving and loading
-----------------------
  * Autosave: whenever the calendar moves (every week, the offseason, a new job), right after
    you choose how to play, and when you quit.
  * [V] on any tab saves under any name. Tab 8 -> [L] (or Load Game on the main menu) loads any
    save; [X#] on that screen deletes one (type DELETE to confirm).
  * Saves live in the saves/ folder next to the game. They're written to a temp file first,
    so a crash mid-save never wipes the last good one. A save keeps every finished game's
    box score; each archived season adds about 3 MB.
  * Older saves load in newer versions: new systems fill themselves in (stadiums are broken
    into parts, recruits get high schools, and so on).
  * Export to Sheets (tab 8 -> [E]): writes the whole league to one formatted Excel workbook in
    the exports/ folder (it opens in Google Sheets too: File > Import > Upload). An Overview tab
    links to every sheet. It holds every team, ranking, schedule and game; every FBS roster with
    all the player info (ratings, all 11 position fits, bio, injury, morale, NIL, traits), stats,
    rating history and coaches; the full recruit database, who is after whom, every board, class
    rankings and signing history; and your own decisions (weekly plans, headset calls, recruiting
    actions, standing orders, inbox). [1] adds every player's game-by-game log (a much bigger file);
    [2] leaves out recruits' hidden true ratings for a spoiler-free copy. It needs the openpyxl
    package and offers to install it. Exporting never changes your world. Your recruiting actions
    and weekly decisions are recorded from v20 on, so older saves start those sheets empty.

4.4  Settings (tab 8 -> [S], or Settings on the main menu)
------------------------------------------------------------
  Saved as you change them, and shared by every save.
  GAME DAY
    [1] Campus Countdown show before each week, on/off   [2] Campus Countdown speed (instant, fast, normal)
    [3] The Campus Countdown cast: rename it, or use a fictional crew and show
    [4] Halftime adjustments, on/off            [5] Big moments: what brings you in
    [6] Sideline: the tablet, series check-ins (every / notable / off), the people moments
        (trainer, the quarterback, leadership) and the situational calls (see 8.7-8.12)
    [F] Game view: tablet (auto / wide / compact), field only, slim field, or off (see 8.13)
  YOUR WEEK (COACH CAREER)
    [7] Week hub before your games, on/off      [8] Default weekly routine (7.2)
    [9] Show what answers do (effect lines)     [D] Difficulty (see 3.5)
    [G] Guided first week, on/off (see 32)
  TOOLS
    [C] Auto copy output (see 28.3)

 CHAPTER 5 · PLAYING IN A WINDOW (PLAY.PY) AND THE SIDEBAR
================================================================================================

5.1  The window
---------------
  * Click instead of type. Every [R]-style key on screen is a button. The dashboard tabs are
    clickable.
    Typing still works everywhere; the up arrow brings back what you typed last.
  * Almost every name is a link: players, teams (in any case, and names cut short to fit a
    column), coaches and coordinators, conferences, stadiums, rivalries and trophies, recruits
    and this season's bowls. A click opens its page in a card over the screen you're on. Names
    in the card are links too; Back (or Backspace) returns to the last card, Esc closes.
    FCS opponents have no page, so they aren't links. When two things share a name, the longer
    name wins ("Dallas Bonner" over Dallas), then your team's players, your next opponent's,
    teams, conferences, coaches and everyone else.
  * The arrow buttons look back at the last 40 screens (Alt+Left / Alt+Right). Page Up and
    the mouse scroll back through a long broadcast.
  * A- / A+ (Ctrl - / Ctrl +) change the text size; the screen fits itself to the window.
    There's a light/dark theme, sounds (a touchdown chime and an inbox ping), and Copy Screen.
  * It runs only on your computer (a local page at 127.0.0.1; nothing goes online). Save with
    [V] or quit with [Q] before closing the window.

5.2  The sidebar
----------------
  The sidebar is your season at a glance, live, in your conference's colors.
  At the top: your rank, record, streak, the last five results, season progress, points per
  game and a light that reads YOUR MOVE (the game is waiting for you) or WORKING.

  Five tabs (Alt+1 to Alt+5):
    HOME     the next game (a win-chance dial — an outlook dial in Coach Career — kickoff,
             stadium, how loud it is, the rivalry, the series), your last result, one-click
             SHORTCUTS, the coach card (hot seat and why it moved, contract, goals), an
             inbox preview, and in AD mode your board, fans and boosters.
    GAMES    your poll rank week by week, points for and against, the schedule with results.
    LEAGUE   conference standings, the Media Top 25 with movement, the NP rankings, the
             Golden Helmet watch and the toughest places to play.
    TEAM     the starting lineup (who's filling in for an injured starter), the injury
             report, team leaders, the program (facilities, stadium, fund), the recruiting
             class with your standing orders (what runs, what's cut) and booked official
             visits, and the searchable roster with position filters.
    NEWS     the week's national scoreboard (upsets marked), headlines, the hot seat watch,
             the recruiting wire and your own story.

  Shortcuts type the keys for you, so they only work from the dashboard (they gray out
  anywhere else): play the next week, save, inbox, depth chart, practice report, recruiting
  hub, standing orders, war room, official visits, coaching tree, staff, roster, team page,
  facilities, budget, schedule, scoreboard, standings, polls, Golden Helmet, toughest places,
  portal, carousel and the media center.

  Pop-up notes appear for wins, losses, poll moves and new mail. Click a card's title to fold
  it, drag the sidebar's left edge to resize it; it remembers your choices. The menu button
  hides the sidebar.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 6 · A SEASON FROM START TO FINISH
================================================================================================

6.1  The calendar
-----------------
  PRESEASON (week 0)   Fall camp: your staff sets the depth chart; you set your board, run
                       a summer camp, and can change non-conference games. No recruiting
                       hours yet.
  WEEKS 1-13           The regular season: 12 games and one bye for everyone. Weeks 1-3 are
                       mostly non-conference; conference play fills the rest; Week 13 is
                       Rivalry Week. The recruiting ratings services re-rank recruits in
                       Weeks 5, 9 and 13.
  WEEK 14              Conference Championships.
  WEEK 15              NP First Round (on campus) and the EARLY SIGNING PERIOD.
  WEEK 16              Bowl season and the NP Quarterfinals.
  WEEK 17              NP Semifinals.
  WEEK 18              The National Championship.
  THE OFFSEASON        Awards, the draft, the coaching carousel, the staff carousel,
                       development, the transfer portal, National Signing Day, realignment,
                       and a new schedule. See Chapter 24.

6.2  Kickoff times and TV
-------------------------
  Every game has a date and an Eastern kickoff time: Thursday and Friday nights, a Labor Day
  game and a Sunday night game on opening weekend, Lake Country Lights on weeknights in November,
  Thanksgiving and Black Friday on Rivalry Week, and Saturday's noon, 3:30 and primetime
  windows. The week's biggest game gets national primetime, the West Coast plays late,
  Hawai'i kicks off at midnight Eastern. Games are played in that order.

6.3  The week's games
---------------------
  [A] plays the week. Games come up in kickoff order, biggest matchups first. For each game
  that isn't yours: [C] commentary (watch it with the booth), [F] fast sim, [W] sim the rest
  of the week. Fast-sim-to-end always stops at your game. The results screen opens any box
  score by number.

6.4  Campus Countdown
--------------------
  Before the games each week, the Campus Countdown crew goes live from the week's biggest campus
  (never the same one twice a season). The show is built from your world's actual storylines:
  rivalries, unbeaten clashes, revenge games, hot seats, Golden Helmet players, streaks, poll moves,
  injuries, league and playoff races. There are signs, a tailgate segment, a one-on-one, the
  picks, a guest picker and the headgear pick. [Enter] continues, [S] skips to the picks,
  [X] skips the show. Settings turns it off or speeds it up. It never changes a result.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 7 · YOUR WEEK (COACH CAREER)
================================================================================================

7.1  Fall camp (preseason)
--------------------------
  [A] from the dashboard before Week 1 opens fall camp:
    * your schedule with a projected win total (words in Coach Career),
    * the AD's expectations, your goals and the captains' check-in,
    * your recruiting board and inbox,
    * a DEPTH CHART line: your staff has built this year's chart; it shows how many new
      starters there are and whether they suggest any position moves ([D] to look).
  Keys: [Enter] kick off Week 1, [D] depth chart, [C] camp report, [S] change your
  non-conference schedule, [R] recruits, [I] inbox, [B] back.

  Custom schedule ([S]): pick any non-conference game and choose a new opponent that week —
  Power, Group of Five or FCS — with each one's strength and your odds shown. Conference
  games, protected rivalries and Rivalry Week can't move. A Power program won't play at a
  Group of Five stadium. The schedule you build is the one the AD, the poll and the
  committee judge you on.

7.2  The week hub
-----------------
  Before each of your games, one screen covers the week:
    MONDAY · FILM        what the tape says about your opponent ([O] film on their players)
    TUESDAY · INBOX      who's reached out
    WEDNESDAY · PRACTICE the week's focus — each one a trade-off:
        Game-plan heavy      +0.8 Saturday, injuries x1.25
        Balanced             +0.4
        Ball security        +0.5, steadier late
        Situational football +0.3, much sharper in close fourth quarters
        Physical, full pads  +0.7, injuries x1.3
        Rest & recover       no lift, injuries x0.75, short injuries heal a week sooner
        Recruiting week      -0.3 Saturday, +8 recruiting hours
        Weather prep         +0.3, and the weather hits your team half as hard (Chapter 10)
    THURSDAY · PRESS     optional; two questions from the week you're actually having
    FRIDAY · INJURIES    who's out, and your game plan ([G], see 7.5)
  The SATURDAY panel carries the forecast; when the weather will matter, the staff says so on
  the FILM panel and suggests Weather prep. Enter goes straight to Saturday. [R] opens the
  practice report, [W] the Weather Center. Settings [7] turns the hub off.

  ROUTINES — the week in one key. A routine is a practice focus, a game plan and the
  openers, saved. Press its number on the hub and the whole week is set:
    [1] Standard      the staff's focus (Weather prep when it's coming), the film's plan
    [2] Big game      Game-plan heavy, the film's plan, openers scripted
    [3] Banged up     Rest & recover, the film's plan
    [4] Recruiting    Recruiting week, the film's plan
    [5] Close games   Situational football, the film's plan
    [6]-[8]           yours: [Y] saves this week's setup (name it), makes any routine your
                      default (D#), or deletes one of yours (X#)
  "The film's plan" is the pair of keys your staff recommends against whoever you play, so
  a routine works every week; a key you picked by hand is saved as that key. Your DEFAULT
  routine is loaded when the hub opens (the ROUTINE line says which, and "(adjusted)" once
  you change something), and it's what simmed weeks and a hub turned off use — otherwise
  they use your usual practice focus. Leave the hub and come back: it's as you left it.

  After the game, THE WEEK'S WORK credits every decision (see 8.11).

7.3  Press conferences and the podium
-------------------------------------
  Every question has three answers, and every answer shows its upside and its cost first.
  The tones:
    confident   recruits like it — as an underdog it's bulletin-board material for them
    measured    composed late — but no headlines, and some ADs want noise
    fiery       +0.3 Saturday, emotional late; some ADs love it, some wince
    humble      +0.15, takes heat off your players; doesn't sell to recruits
  Many answers do more (backing your QB, standing by a coordinator, a shot at the rival).
  Some draw a follow-up ("are you guaranteeing it?"). Repeating the same tone gets half the
  upside. After games, a postgame podium asks two or three questions about the game you just
  played; answers carry into next week.

7.4  Your inbox
---------------
  [I] from anywhere. People react to what actually happened, in their own voice: your AD,
  players, recruits, staff, media, boosters, donors, legends, the Recruiting Office (a
  weekly recruiting report). Messages that want an answer give three or four real choices,
  none free. The colored line under each option says what it does: green what you get, red
  what it costs, yellow a risk or something riding on a later result. A reply can touch your
  AD's patience, the locker room, this Saturday, late-game poise, injury risk, recruiting
  hours and interest, next year's NIL money, a coordinator's loyalty, or one player's mood.
  Leave a message two weeks and it closes — and some people notice. Settings [9] hides the
  effect lines if you'd rather play by feel.

7.5  The game plan (Friday)
---------------------------
  [G] on the week hub. Your staff tells you what the film shows ("their corners are the soft
  spot", "their QB can hurt you with his legs"), then you pick one key on each side:
    OFFENSE   Balanced · Establish the run · Attack them deep · Quick game vs their rush ·
              Ball control
    DEFENSE   Balanced · Stop the run · Take away their best receiver · Get after their QB ·
              Keep everything in front · Contain the quarterback
  Each key is rated against this opponent: a real edge, should help, fighting uphill, or the
  film says no. A key that fits the matchup is worth more; one that doesn't is worth less,
  and every key has a cost somewhere else (stop the run and the deep ball gets a little
  easier; get after the QB and draws hurt you). Game-plan heavy practice sharpens both keys.
  [S] scripts your openers: your offense is sharp for its first ten snaps, but scripting
  costs a practice period (-0.1 Saturday). [R] takes the staff's plan. Skip the screen and
  your staff's plan is used. Every CPU staff game-plans too — the better ones read film better.
  At halftime you see whether each key is working.

7.6  December
-------------
  Each postseason week is set a week early, so a title game, bowl or playoff game gets a full
  week of prep, press and mail: championship week, the bowl trip (business or reward),
  draft-bound players asking to opt out, the early signing period, and the unhappy players
  you might lose to the portal.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 8 · GAME DAY: THE WAYS TO PLAY A GAME
================================================================================================

8.1  Your matchup screen
------------------------
  The date and TV window, both teams (records, strength, schemes, aggression), the stadium,
  the expected crowd, how loud the building is and its toughest-places rank, the rivalry,
  the series and the trophy. Then choose:
    [1] Sim it                 straight to the final, with the scoring summary
    [2] Play the big moments   the staff calls it; you take the headset when it matters
    [3] Coach every snap       every play is yours
    [4] Watch the broadcast    full commentary, no play-calling
  The game remembers your last choice, speed, and whether you call defense.

8.2  Coaching a snap
--------------------
  Before every snap on offense you choose a run or pass from the full playbook (a star marks
  your scheme's plays), or the staff's call (always shown as a suggestion). You decide fourth
  downs (go, punt, field goal, or [X] fake punt / [Z] fake field goal — with the staff's read
  on whether they'll be ready for it, and what the fourth-down chart says), kneels, timeouts
  (a timeout gets back the clock the last play burned) and tempo (hurry-up, normal, milk the
  clock). On defense, pick the call or hand it to your coordinator; at home on third down,
  [C] gets the crowd up. [B] opens SUBSTITUTIONS on either side: switch one player with another
  player in his position room, or use mass subs to put the backup units in (and bring the starters
  back later). [G] opens your orders on either side (8.8). [S] sim ahead gives the headset to your
  staff for the drive, the quarter, the half or the game.

8.3  Big moments
----------------
  The staff calls the game and the booth stays quiet until a snap that decides it: a real
  fourth-down decision, goal-to-go, a two-minute drill, a goal-line stand, every snap of a
  one-score fourth quarter, overtime. Each moment opens with a recap of what you missed.
  Settings [5] chooses what counts: overtime, crunch time (minutes and margin), fourth downs,
  the two-minute drill, the red zone, goal-line stands, their late fourth downs, and the
  blowout margin past which the staff plays it out.

8.4  Quarter breaks and halftime
--------------------------------
  At every quarter break: skip the next quarter, skip to the final, change speed (1 slow to
  4 instant), toggle the coach's view (both play calls on every snap), or switch between
  big moments and every snap.
  Halftime (every game you coach, simmed or played): the first-half numbers, whether your
  game-plan keys are working (7.5), and what your staff sees, then a second-half plan — trust the staff, lean on the run, air it out, bring
  pressure, sit back in coverage, protect the lead, go for broke — and a message: let the
  coordinators talk, calm and clear, or light into them (odds shown). Settings [4] turns it
  off.

8.5  Who calls the plays
------------------------
  Every head coach calls his offense, his defense, both or neither. The play-caller's scheme
  is what the team runs and recruits to. Yours defaults to you calling both; change it on
  My Staff (tab 6 -> [S]) or before any game. Hand the offense to your OC and the headset
  skips offensive snaps (fourth downs stay yours).

8.6  The broadcast and the box score
------------------------------------
  A two-person booth calls every game in natural language: the stakes, the series, the
  crowd, the weather, the players' stories, the trophy after the final. A full box score
  follows every game. Box scores are kept forever: open one from the week's results, any
  scoreboard (Schedules & Scores: W7 jumps to week 7, N/P step through weeks), any team's
  schedule, or Past Seasons.

8.7  The tablet
---------------
  Under every call, the sideline tablet:
    * momentum (them ━━━●━━━ us) and the crowd — when the building has turned on you, the
      staff says a timeout would settle it, and it does (8.10)
    * this drive, your standing orders, and whether you still have your challenge
    * what's working and what isn't today (quick game 7/9, outside runs 1/6), or on defense
      what's hurting you (deep shots 11.5 a play, 2 big)
    * what they do in this down and distance, from what you've seen today: their defense on
      3rd & long (blitz 4 of 6, man 5 of 6, two-deep, eight in the box) or their offense
      on 1st down (run 7 of 9, mostly outside runs)
    * one line from your coordinator: the read, and usually a play that beats it ("They've
      sent pressure on 3rd & long 4 of 6. Slants beats it."), your own tendencies ("We've
      run on 1st down 8 of 10. They know it."), and what the other sideline just changed
      ("Their DC just brought a safety down.")
    * who's hot and who's cold, and anyone playing hurt
  The other staff reads you the same way: run on first down every time and their box creeps
  up; torch one receiver and he gets doubled.

8.8  Series orders
------------------
  Between series your coordinator comes over (Settings [6]: every series, only when he has
  something — the default — or never; [G] any time). He says what he sees, and you set the
  orders for that side of the ball. Orders stand until you change them.
    OFFENSE   Normal · Max protect (a back stays in to block: more time, one fewer receiver)
              · Quick game (ball out fast: fewer sacks, fewer deep shots) · Pound it (run it:
              it wears on them, and they'll load up) · Tempo (no huddle: their defense can't
              sub and tires on long drives; the clock moves) · Take shots (downfield: big
              plays, more sacks and picks)
              [F] feed a player: his route comes first in the QB's reads, or he gets the
              carries. Keep feeding him and they'll key on him.
    DEFENSE   Normal · Spy the QB (one fewer rusher) · Two deep (nothing over the top, softer
              against the run) · Load the box (a safety down: stop the run, exposed deep) ·
              Pin your ears back (more pressure; draws and screens hurt) · Double their best
              WR (everybody else is one-on-one)
  Orders shape what your staff calls, and most of them change the snap itself, even when
  you pick the play. If the OC or DC calls a side, orders are how you steer him.

8.9  The people
---------------
  TRAINER'S REPORT   A starter goes down with something short of a season-ender. Shut him
                     down; tape it and send him back (he plays at about 80%, and every hit
                     is a chance to make it worse: out twice as long, plus a week); or hold
                     him for the fourth quarter, when he comes back only if it's close.
  THE QUARTERBACK    Two picks, or a day like 4 of 14: the QB coach says so. [Q] in the
                     check-in: the backup for a series, or for the rest of the day (the
                     starter takes that hard). Later: put him back in.
  ON THE SIDELINE    After a pick, a lost fumble, a costly flag, a second drop, or a blown
                     coverage, the player comes off rattled — on both teams. You choose:
                     put an arm around him, get in his face, let a captain handle it, or sit
                     him a series. His position coach tells you who he is. Young players and
                     head cases need the arm; gamers and tough guys answer the challenge;
                     get in a diva's face and it backfires. Cold-blooded veterans don't need
                     either. Two a quarter at most.

8.10  The calls that were the staff's
-------------------------------------
  THE TRY            After your touchdown: kick, go for two (staff's play), or go for two and
                     pick the play yourself. The staff follows the two-point chart. (From the
                     second overtime on, the rules make everyone go for two.)
  KICKOFFS           Deep, squib, onside, or a surprise onside when they aren't expecting it
                     (about 45%; the second one in a game, much less).
  FLAGS              When the choice is close — holding on a play that went nowhere, holding
                     on third down, defensive holding on a short gain — you accept or decline.
  THE CHALLENGE      On a close catch, a fumble that might have been a knee down, or a spot
                     a yard short on third or fourth down, your guy upstairs gives you his
                     read. One challenge a game; win it and you keep it; lose it and it costs a
                     timeout. CPU staffs challenge too.
  ICE THE KICKER     Before their field goal late in a close game, a timeout makes him wait.
                     It gets to young kickers and head cases; cold-blooded ones don't care.
  STOP THE BLEEDING  A timeout when momentum has swung hard against you cools it off.
  THE CROWD          At home on their third down, [C] gets the building up: more false
                     starts, a little extra for your defense. Do it every time and they tire of
                     being asked.
  OVERTIME           Win the toss and you choose; the staff says defense first.
  Settings [6] can hand all of these back to the staff.

8.11  After the game: the week's work, the headset log and the grade
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE WEEK'S WORK (after every game, simmed or coached). Each decision you made that week,
  credited in rating points of game-day form and in points on the scoreboard (about 1.7 a
  rating point):
    WED  the practice focus, and what came of its side effects — injuries against its
         injury rate, the weather it prepared you for (or didn't), late poise and whether
         there was a one-score fourth quarter to use it in, recruiting hours, who came
         back a week early
    THU  the press conference (or "skipped"), TUE your inbox replies, LAST last week's
         postgame podium, ALL anything that lasts the season; bulletin-board material
         for the other side counts against you
    FRI  the game plan: each key, how well it fit, and whether it worked over the whole
         game; the scripted openers (snaps, yards, first downs, touchdowns)
  Then the total, set against the margin ("You won by 3. The week was worth about 4 — it
  was the difference."). The hub shows last week's line and your season total.

  THE HEADSET (games you coached). Every fourth down, try, kickoff call, flag, challenge,
  icing, trainer's call, quarterback change and sideline talk, what happened — and what
  each call was WORTH when you made it, in win chance. The model knows the score, the
  clock, your kicker, the matchup and the day's form, and it's calibrated to this game's
  engine (1,500 simulated games): how often drives score from each spot, how often a
  fourth-and-X converts (4th & 1-2 81%, 3 73%, 4 60%, 5 47%, 7-9 ~26%, 10+ 16%), how often
  two-point tries work (about 68%), onside and surprise onside odds. Each call reads:
    ✓ right call        within 1.2 points of win chance of the best option
    ★ right call        ...and the chart said otherwise
    ≈ close call        every option within 1.2 points: anything goes, not graded
    ✗ −3.4%             win chance given away, and the better call
  with the options' win chances under the big ones. The challenge is priced from what
  your guy upstairs told you, not from the replay; sideline talk from the odds you were
  shown, not the coin flip.

  THE PROCESS GRADE (A+ to F) is the weighted average of those calls: +1 for a right call,
  0 at about 2 points of win chance given away, -1 at about 4. Late calls and bigger ones
  weigh a little more; a call where the chart itself was wrong counts double. Following the
  chart is fine when the chart is right — it usually is — but it's no longer an automatic
  A: the chart doesn't know the score, the clock or your kicker, and the log tells you how
  often it was wrong and what your calls were worth against simply following it. The
  result never counts. A game where nothing was riding on any call gets no grade. Losing a
  one-score game you had the headset for, with two timeouts in your pocket, still costs
  you. The podium asks about the call that decided it: the fourth-and-3 you went for, the
  punt the chart wouldn't have.

8.12  Big moments and the sideline
----------------------------------
  In Play the big moments, the tablet, the orders, the people and the calls come with the
  headset: when the staff has the game, they handle all of it. The try and kickoffs only come
  to you late. The trainer always asks, unless you've handed the staff the rest of the game.

8.13  The tablet: the field, the play-by-play and the numbers
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Before every snap of a game you watch or coach, the game is drawn as a tablet:

    * SCOREBOARD on top: both scores in big digits, the quarter and clock, the down and distance,
      the ball's spot, timeouts (filled dots are left) and the weather.
    * THE FIELD in the middle, with the ball where the game says it is: yard lines, the line of
      scrimmage, the yellow first-down marker (gone at goal to go), the drive so far as a trail, and
      the end zone the offense is attacking lit orange. The away team's end zone is on the left all
      game and the home team's on the right. RED ZONE and GOAL TO GO show up under it.
    * PLAY BY PLAY down the side, newest first: what the booth called on each snap, with the quarter,
      clock and down. Kickoffs and reviews between snaps get their own entries.
    * SCORE BY QUARTER and THIS DRIVE (plays, yards, time, how far to the chains).
    * COACH'S CLIPBOARD in a game you coach: momentum and crowd, your orders, what's working, their
      defense's tendencies on this down, your coordinator's whisper, who's hot and cold. Watching a
      broadcast, that spot shows TOP PERFORMERS and SCORING instead.
    * TEAM STATS along the bottom: yards, yards/play, success rate, rush, pass, explosive plays,
      first downs, third/fourth downs, red-zone TDs, turnovers, penalties and possession, with
      the leader in each column in bold. Explosive = a 10+ yard run or 20+ yard pass.

  Two sizes. WIDE is 124 columns with the play-by-play beside the field; COMPACT is 100 columns
  with the panels stacked under it. AUTO (the default) picks WIDE when the terminal reports 124
  columns or more, and COMPACT otherwise (always in the window, whatever terminal launched it).
  Pick WIDE yourself in the window and the text shrinks so all 124 columns fit. Colors are needed.

  Settings [F] cycles: Tablet (auto size) · Tablet, wide · Tablet, compact · Field only (just the
  field under the situation line) · Slim field (one row) · Off (the plain ticker line). The tablet
  is only ever drawn once per snap, and it changes nothing about the game itself.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 9 · HOW A GAME IS SIMULATED
================================================================================================

  Every snap is played out. Ratings in a game are "applied" fundamentals: the raw skill times
  the player's proficiency at the position he's lining up at.

  * Play calling: each coach's scheme, aggression and personal lean (plus a few signature
    calls). Staffs adapt in-game: go back to what works, punish blitzes with screens, load
    the box against the run, spy mobile quarterbacks, bracket star receivers, adjust at the
    half. 53 offensive plays (including trick plays and fakes) and 20 defensive calls.
  * Runs: point of attack (linemen vs the defender at the gap, doubles, pullers, box count),
    second level, the alley and the secondary, the open field.
  * Passes: each rusher vs his blocker sets the time to pressure; each route has a depth and
    a break; separation comes from route running vs coverage and scheme matchups. The QB
    reads in order at a speed set by his IQ, climbs, escapes, scrambles, throws it away, or
    takes the sack. Throws resolve from accuracy, arm, window and pressure.
  * Position skills matter: a QB with a bad deep ball misses deep; a corner with poor deep
    cover gets beaten over the top; a punter with placement pins you inside the 20.
  * Momentum: one meter (-100 to +100) moves on big plays and bleeds back toward zero; at most
    it's worth about 2 rating points, halftime wipes most of it, blowouts barely feed it.
  * The crowd is the home-field edge: a packed, loud building is worth about three times what
    a half-empty one is, and road offenses jump offside. Neutral sites have no home team.
  * Injuries: every contact carries a risk scaled by durability — shaken up, out for the game,
    1-3 games, 4-8 games, or the season. The practice focus, your stadium's playing surface
    and some coaching-tree perks change the odds. Everyone is healthy by fall camp.
  * Game day form: each team gets a small random swing for the day; locker-room chemistry and
    morale add a little; blowouts get the starters pulled.

  * Weather: every outdoor game is played in real conditions — rain, snow, wind, cold, heat,
    altitude — that change quarter by quarter. See Chapter 10.

  Across thousands of games it lands on FBS norms: about 26-28 points, 70 plays and 5.2 yards
  per play per team, 64% completions, a couple of sacks. Evenly matched teams split; a 4-8
  point rating edge wins about 80% of the time; home teams win about 57%.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 10 · WEATHER: FORECASTS, CONDITIONS AND WHAT THEY DO
================================================================================================

  Every outdoor game is played in real weather, built from where and when it kicks off. It
  changes during the game, it shows up in the forecast days before, and it changes how the
  game is played — for both teams, in every game, simmed or watched.

10.1  Where the weather comes from
----------------------------------
  * Climate: every state (and towns with weather of their own — Buffalo's lake snow,
    Laramie's wind, Seattle's rain, Fresno's heat, the Rockies' altitude) has a September
    normal and a December normal, a rain rate, a typical wind, humidity and a snow factor.
    The season cools week by week. Noon kickoffs are the warmest; night games are colder,
    and more so in dry climates.
  * Weather systems: each region shares a week's pattern — a warm spell, a cold snap, a wet
    week — so one front can soak a whole conference. About one Saturday in six a front comes
    through during the game: the wind swings around and picks up, and the temperature drops.
  * Tropical storms: most seasons one or two come ashore on the Gulf or Atlantic coast in
    September or October. Every game in their path gets heavy rain and 20-40 mph wind all
    day. The Weather Center posts an alert the week before, and it makes the headlines.
  * Where the game is played: the home stadium, or the neutral site or bowl city. Domes and
    closed roofs (Syracuse, San Antonio, the Pro League domes that host title games and bowls) have no
    weather at all: 72 degrees and a dry ball.

10.2  During the game
---------------------
  Conditions are set quarter by quarter:
  * Rain or snow can be falling at kickoff, move in later, pick up, or clear out. Steady rain
    can last all day; summer thunderstorms are short and heavy. Below freezing it snows;
    around 33-36 degrees it's sleet.
  * Thunderstorms sometimes bring a LIGHTNING DELAY: the officials clear the field for
    30-90 minutes. When play resumes, momentum is back to zero.
  * The temperature falls after sunset and drops fast behind a front.
  * The wind has a direction and a speed. Teams switch ends every quarter, so a 20 mph wind
    helps one team's kicks and deep balls in the first and third quarters, and the other
    team's in the second and fourth.
  * The field: rain soaks it and snow piles on it. Turf drains; natural grass turns sloppy,
    then muddy. Heated hybrid grass (a stadium part — Chapter 17) drains fast and snow won't
    stick. A worn or sloppy field takes speed out of the game.
  The booth tells you when it changes ("That rain has turned to snow"), and the box score
  lists the conditions for every quarter.

10.3  What it does on the field
-------------------------------
  Rain and snow   passes are less accurate and harder to catch, more drops, more fumbles
                  and muffed punts, kickers lose distance and footing
  Wind            deep balls and kicks drift; a head wind can take 10+ yards off a field
                  goal, a tail wind adds a few; crosswinds push kicks wide; punts carry
                  with it and die into it; kickoffs reach the end zone less often into it
  Cold            the ball gets hard: fewer catches, shorter kicks; teams from warm climates
                  (and dome teams) play a little worse in the cold
  Heat            in the second half, teams that don't live in it wilt a little
  Altitude        kicks fly farther above about 3,000 feet; visitors from sea level tire late
                  at 4,500 feet and up (Laramie, Colorado Springs, Boulder, Albuquerque, Utah)
  A sloppy field  big runs dry up: speed matters less when nobody can cut
  In a real weather game expect fewer points and passing yards, more carries, and more
  fumbles. Coaches adjust: they run more, take fewer deep shots (unless the gale is at
  their back), and punt instead of trying a long field goal into the wind — the staff's
  field-goal range moves with the wind every quarter.
  Crowds: rain, cold and heat keep some fans home (a losing season makes it worse; a roof
  canopy over the stands keeps them dry). Big games still sell out.

10.4  Forecasts
---------------
  Forecasts are honest but uncertain. The farther out, the closer they are to "normal for
  the date"; the week of the game they're close. A 40% chance of rain means it rains in
  about four of every ten of those games. A front, a cold snap or a storm may only show up
  in the forecast the week of the game.
  Where to see them:
    * the NEXT GAME panel on HOME (games two weeks out or closer),
    * tab 2 SCHEDULE: the next-game forecast, and [F] the Weather Center,
    * your full schedule: a forecast beside the next three games, the conditions beside
      played games that had real weather,
    * the week hub (Coach Career): the SATURDAY panel, a staff note on the FILM panel when
      the weather will matter ("the wind will be a factor in the kicking game"), and [W],
    * the window's sidebar: the forecast on the next-game card, and a Weather center
      shortcut.

10.5  The Weather Center (tab 2 -> [F], or [W] on the week hub)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  YOUR NEXT GAME   the forecast: kickoff temperature and its likely range, the chance of
                   rain, snow or storms, the wind, normal for the date, and how confident
                   the forecast is; the field surface, your home climate, altitude
  EXTENDED OUTLOOK your next three games after that
  ALERTS           tropical storms coming ashore this week or next
  [C] around the country   the week's forecasts, the weather games first
  [L] last week's weather games   the scores, the conditions, what happened
  [K] weather book   your program's games in real weather: record in rain, snow, wind, cold
                     and heat, the coldest and hottest, every one with its score

10.6  Preparing for it (Coach Career)
-------------------------------------
  Weather prep is a practice focus (the week hub's [P]): wet balls, kicking into the wind,
  hand-offs in gloves. It's worth +0.3 on Saturday, and the weather hits your team half as
  hard: accuracy, catching, fumbles, muffs, kicking, and the cold, heat and altitude. When
  the forecast is bad, your staff suggests it.
  Weather also follows your recruits: an official visit in a downpour or a sleet storm
  lands softer (much softer with a kid from Florida or Texas), a snow game thrills a
  northern kid, and a perfect fall Saturday helps.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 11 · YOUR ROSTER: WHAT YOU SEE, DEPTH CHARTS, PRACTICE, DEVELOPMENT, MORALE
================================================================================================

11.1  The staff's eye, not the numbers (Coach Career)
-----------------------------------------------------
  In Coach Career you never see a real rating. Players "look like" a long shot,
  developmental, backup, rotation player, starter, quality starter, star or All-American.
  Skills read horrible / bad / below average / average / good / great / elite. Teams read
  very weak to elite; odds read "slight underdog", "toss-up", "heavy favorite". Stats always
  show. (Spectator, AD mode and simmed seasons show every number.) The Scout's Eye perk adds
  a narrow estimate next to every word.

  Short codes in tables: LS long shot · DEV developmental · BU backup · ROT rotation ·
  STR starter · QS quality starter · STAR · AA All-American.

11.2  Players
-------------
  Every player has fundamentals (speed, strength, IQ, hands, and so on), a proficiency at
  every position (0.1-1.2; his overall at a spot is the relevant fundamentals times that
  proficiency), position sub-skills, one to three personality traits, a mood, a potential,
  and a history. His card ([P] and a jersey number, or click his name in the window) shows
  it all plus a staff scouting report ("accurate on quick throws, but can't push the ball
  downfield").

  Position sub-skills: QB short / medium / deep accuracy, pressure, dual threat · RB inside,
  outside, receiving, pass pro, ball security · WR short routes, deep routes, hands, YAC ·
  TE run block, routes, hands, YAC · OL run block, pass pro, space · DL pass rush, run
  defense, pursuit · LB run fits, blitz, coverage, tackling · CB short and deep cover, ball
  skills, tackling · S deep help, man cover, run support, ball skills · K accuracy, range ·
  P distance, placement.

11.3  Traits
------------
  59 player traits in opposing pairs: Clutch, Big-Game Player, Cold Blooded, Slow Starter,
  Flat-Track Bully, Gamer, Practice All-American, Warrior, Glass, Fast Healer, Film Junkie,
  Late Bloomer, Coachable, Stubborn, Captain, Hothead, Homesick, Chip On His Shoulder, Diva,
  Showman, Road Warrior, Home Cooking, Rivalry Guy, November Player and more. They tilt
  game-day form, the fourth quarter of close games, injuries, growth, penalties, and whether a
  player enters the portal. Nothing swings a game alone; over a season the tilt shows.

11.4  The depth chart (tab 3 -> [H])
------------------------------------
  EVERY PRESEASON YOUR STAFF BUILDS IT. When a new season starts (and when you take a job),
  your staff builds the whole chart from scratch with the same eye as the practice report:
  last year's starters keep a small edge, starters per position follow your scheme's base
  personnel, and a true freshman who isn't starting sits behind a close veteran to save his
  redshirt. They also list any position moves they'd make (a room short on bodies, or a
  deep backup who'd start somewhere else). After that, the chart is yours; the staff never
  touches it during the season.

  On the depth chart screen:
    [N]/[P]        next / previous position (or type a position: TE)
    [M a b]        move slot a to slot b (M 3 1 makes the third man the starter)
    [C # POS]      move a player to a new position (C 82 OL) — he's worse there at first
                   and improves with reps and an offseason
    [S1] [S2]...   take one of the staff's suggested position moves
    [R]            use the staff's recommendation at this position
    [W]            the practice report
  Injured starters keep their spot and get it back when healthy; until then the next man
  plays.

  FORMATION SUBS. [U] on your team page, [F] in fall camp/the week hub, or Formation/play
  packages from the in-game substitutions menu opens the same standing package board. Set
  skill-position overrides for 10, 11, 12, 21 or 22 personnel, then optionally override a
  specific play. The personnel package is applied first and the play-specific instruction
  second. If that player is unavailable, the normal depth chart fills in. These packages are
  saved with the team and are meant to be built during the week, not only on Saturday.

  Other programs' staffs manage their charts all year: a fresh chart every preseason, a
  weekly re-sort as players develop (a backup who has clearly passed a starter takes the job;
  a starter doesn't lose it over a point), and position moves when they need them.

11.5  The practice report (tab 3 -> [P], [R] on the week hub, [C] in fall camp)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Every week every player puts up a practice line: team-period completions and picks for
  quarterbacks, one-on-ones won for linemen and receivers, drops, field goals by distance.
  Lines come from real skills plus luck, so a great player can have a bad Wednesday. The
  report names position battles and gives the staff's recommended depth chart ([A] accepts
  it). Your staff's eye is the blend of your Coach Skill and your coordinators; a sharp staff
  reads close to the truth. Veterans are read better than freshmen, and the read sharpens as
  the season goes on.

11.6  Development
-----------------
  About a third of a player's yearly growth happens during the season, week by week; two
  thirds happen in the offseason. What drives it: his potential and age, your staff's
  development ratings for his position (the head coach and the coordinator on his side of
  the ball), your training facilities, his traits, whether he fit what he wanted when he
  signed (the fit bonus), and a little luck.

  REDSHIRT PLAN. Fall camp -> [X] lets you protect an eligible player before Week 1. A
  protected player is held out of ordinary lineup selection so the CPU cannot casually burn
  his year. It is still a plan, not a magic lock: an emergency can force him onto the field,
  and deliberately assigning him to a formation package makes him available. At season end,
  four games or fewer preserves the year; a fifth game burns the plan.

11.7  Morale, chemistry, captains
---------------------------------
  Every player has a mood (0-100) on his card. Playing time, results, the locker room, his
  NIL deal, who he is, and every call you make about him move it. Under 40 he's shopping the
  portal; under 25 he's very likely gone; above 75 he's staying. The starters' average is
  worth about half a point on Saturday.
  Locker-room chemistry (0-100; tab 3 -> [T] -> [K]) is worth up to about a point either way:
  winning warms it, losing streaks and blowups cool it, leaders hold it together. Every
  August the team votes three captains; you can accept, add one, or overrule. Captains pull
  the room toward their mood; unhappy wild cards drag their position group.
  Incidents happen: suspensions, players venting after losses, practice fights. On your team,
  they land in your inbox and you decide.

11.8  Promises and in-season retention
-----------------------------------------
  [M] on a recruit's card promises him a starting job, real snaps, or a redshirt to develop.
  The card shows his position room next fall first. The promise follows him to campus and is
  graded after his first season: kept (a big morale lift, recruits at his position hear it)
  or broken (his morale craters, his high school goes cold on you, recruits hear it). Your
  kept/broken record makes the next promise worth more or less.

  During the season, Transfer Watch -> [R] (or [K] on the week hub) is your RETENTION board.
  Your staff tells you what it believes is driving the risk. You can listen, reassure him,
  make a specific role promise, or tell him to earn it. Role promises are not flavor text:
  designed package = 8 snaps/game, rotation role = 18 snaps/game, and featured touches for
  RB/WR/TE = 6 carries/targets per game, measured over the next three games. Formation subs
  and the depth chart are how you make those promises real. Kept promises lower portal risk;
  a broken promise can leave him more likely to go than before the conversation.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 12 · YOUR STAFF, YOUR COACHING TREE, AND GROWING AS A COACH
================================================================================================

12.1  Coordinators
------------------
  Every program has an offensive and a defensive coordinator — real coaches with ratings,
  traits and ceilings who can grow into head coaches. Each carries part of the load:
    * games: his side's in-game adjustments and about a third of the nerve,
    * development: 40% of the development rating for players on his side of the ball,
    * recruiting: 20% of the staff's hours and pull, plus a boost with recruits from his home
      region.
  Every season goes on his resume, graded against the talent he had.

12.2  Your staff (tab 6 -> [S])
-------------------------------
  Every offseason a staff review shows how each coordinator's unit did against its talent:
  keep him or let him go. When you take a new job, you decide which inherited coordinators
  stay. Your coordinators can be hired away. You can hand the staff to your AD, or take it
  back.

  THE COORDINATOR SEARCH. Every opening is a short, competitive search ([?] on the board
  explains it in the game):
    BOARD       twelve names: ratings, potential, recruiting, last unit vs talent, scheme, and
                whether he calls plays now. THE READ is public buzz (marked ?) until your
                staff talks to his camp. A star marks coaches who've worked with you.
    [C#] CALL   5 per opening. His real interest, his personality, and what he cares about
                most (money, calling plays, years, a bigger stage, a title, a path to a head
                job, loyalty).
    [I#] INTERVIEW  3 per opening. What's pulling him toward the job and what's holding him
                back, and his camp's terms: opening salary, minimum years, play-calling, the
                assistant head coach title, an out-clause for head-coaching jobs. A coach who
                isn't interested declines the interview. You must interview a coach before
                offering, unless he's worked with you or is already on your staff.
    [O#] OFFER  a package: salary, years, play-calling, the title, the out-clause. He accepts,
                counters with specific terms ([Y] take it), or names the sticking points. A
                lowball sours him; three rounds and he's out of the search. His minimum and
                priorities are fixed for the search, so offering a few dollars more never
                re-rolls an answer, and coaches who decline stay out if the job reopens.
  What moves interest: your program's stature for his level, the step from his current job, a
  jump to a Power league, a first coordinator job, ties to you, his alma mater, his home
  region (Homebodies), personality, how hot YOUR seat is (staffs get fired together), whether
  he just took his current job, and scheme fit (unless he calls the plays, he coaches yours).
  The market moves: each call, interview and offer takes days, and good coaches you haven't
  talked to can commit to staying put or drop out. Once he says yes, his school may match;
  you get one chance to top it. Only then does your AD have to find the money — if it can't
  be found, the deal dies but nothing was cut. The package is real: play-calling goes to him,
  the title is his, and the out-clause lowers his buyout and makes head jobs easier to take.
  Take play-calling back from a coordinator you promised it to and he'll start listening to
  other schools. [A] lets your AD make the hire.

12.3  The coaching tree (tab 6 -> [T])
--------------------------------------
  Results earn skill points: the December review (the season, beating expectations, bowls,
  titles, the rival, AD goals, top classes), first-round draft picks, and career milestones.
  Spend them in six branches — RECRUITER, DEVELOPER, MOTIVATOR, TACTICIAN, POLITICIAN,
  PROGRAM BUILDER — each with four tiers that open as you invest (0 / 4 / 7 / 13 points), a
  fork at tier 2 (pick one, lose the other), and a capstone. A few examples:
    Recruiter   Silver Tongue (+8% interest), Film Junkie (film costs 1 hour), Hometown Hero
                or National Brand, Promise Keeper or Flip Artist, Machine (+15% hours).
    Developer   Position Guru, Strength Program, Redshirt Factory or Plug and Play, Walk-on
                Magic, Pro League Pipeline, Everybody Gets Better or Scout's Eye.
    Motivator   Open Door, Captain's Council, Players' Coach or Hard Nosed, Halftime Speech,
                Thick Skin, Run Through a Wall.
    Tactician   Film Room, Clock Manager, Riverboat or Field Position, Halftime Genius,
                Big-Game Coach, Schemer.
    Politician  Media Savvy, Booster Circuit, Teflon or Lightning Rod, Trusted Voice,
                Fundraiser, Face of the Program.
    Builder     Groundbreaker (15% off facilities), Collective Ties (+5% NIL pool), Coaching
                Tree or Scheme Identity, Stadium Experience, Staff Developer, Blueprint
                (facilities never slip, the budget never shrinks).
  The whole tree costs about 110 points; a long career earns 50-70. Taking a new job gives
  you one free respec.

12.4  Developing your coach (tab 6 -> [D])
------------------------------------------
  CPU coaches grow on their own. You grow only through money you earn on the job: your salary
  and bonuses go into your bank after every season, and you turn the bank into ratings —
  Coach Skill and the six development ratings (recruiting, passing, offense, trenches,
  defensive backs, special teams). A point costs $30,000 x 1.09^(rating - 50): about $30K at
  50, $71K at 60, $168K at 70, $398K at 80, $942K at 90. Coach Skill costs 50% more. Each
  rating can rise at most +3 a year (Coach Skill +2). You start with $250K. You can only spend
  while employed; buyout checks count toward career earnings but never reach the bank.

12.5  Earned traits
-------------------
  Your reputation is earned from what your teams do: The Closer, QB Whisperer, Trench Guru,
  Developer, Motivator, Salesman, Culture Builder and more. [C] My Career shows your seat,
  goals, record, reputation, AD trust and story.

  POSITION COACHES AND THE STAFF ROOM
  -----------------------------------
  Under your coordinators are eight position coaches: quarterbacks, running backs, wide
  receivers, tight ends, offensive line, defensive line, linebackers, defensive backs. Each
  has three ratings:
    DEV    how much his players improve (a quarter of their development rating; the head
           coach and coordinator still carry most of it)
    REC    pull with recruits at his positions (up to about 12% more or less interest)
    EVAL   how well he reads his room: sharper (or blurrier) practice reports and staff depth
           charts at his positions
  They grow while they're young, slip as they age, retire, and get hired away by bigger
  programs (CPU staffs replace theirs; yours are your call).

  WHO THEY ARE. Every position coach is a type, and the type shapes his ratings:
    Developer       builds players: high DEV, recruiting so-so
    Recruiter       wins living rooms: high REC (DEV is often mediocre), extra pull in his region
    Technician      coaches the details: high EVAL, the sharpest read on his room
    Players' coach  his room's morale holds up when bad news hits
    Climber         wants a coordinator job yesterday: grows fast, leaves fast
    Loyalist        goes where his coordinator goes, and almost nowhere else
  DEV is a real multiplier on his room's growth: an elite line coach builds lines (about 25%
  faster growth), a poor one costs about 10%. His profile ([V#] in the staff room) says how
  much, lists the players he signed and the coordinators he'd follow. Each recruits one region
  hardest. Hiring is a market now: the board shows INTEREST, [I#] gives you an interview-only look, and
  pursuing a coach starts contact -> interview -> offer. Candidates can say no. Program stature, current
  job, relationships and money matter, so an elite assistant will not casually take a major step down.

  SPECIALTIES AND TRACK RECORD. Each position coach also has a specialty: QB whisperer or
  Line builder (his room grows 8% faster), Pipeline (owns one state in recruiting), Retention
  (his players rarely hit the portal), Film junkie (the sharpest read), Walk-on whisperer
  (walk-ons grow 20% faster), Closer (four- and five-stars listen). Every season his room's
  growth is measured against the national average for that room; three years of it make his
  reputation (Hot name, Proven developer, Steady, Struggling) — it shows on his profile and in
  the hiring list, raises his price, and puts him on coordinator shortlists.

  THEIR GUYS. Coordinators collect assistants who've worked under them. Hire a coordinator
  and he asks to bring his guys (you can say no — he won't love it). Fire one and his guys
  on your staff are unsettled (! in the staff room): they work a little less hard, and some
  walk out when the staff carousel opens — or follow him to his next job. The players he
  signed take it personally (morale down, portal risk up); the same goes, more mildly, for a
  position coach's signees. A players' coach softens that in his room.

  The STAFF ROOM (tab 6 -> [S], or [S] on the offseason calendar) shows everyone:
    [F#]  fire a coordinator (you owe the rest of his deal) or a position coach
    [H#]  hire into an open chair. Coordinators get the full hiring screen and contract
          talks. Position coach candidates: graduate assistants (cheap, raw, room to grow),
          high school and FCS coaches, NFL assistants, analysts, assistants at smaller
          programs, and anyone out of work.
    [P#]  promote a position coach to coordinator on his side of the ball. He brings his
          ratings; the coordinator he replaces is let go; his old room opens.
    [C]   play-calling and the staff autopilot
  PAY: a room of eight coaches at your program's level is part of operations already. Pay
  above that (better coaches) comes out of the money for players; a cheaper room adds to it.
  A room left empty after the offseason gets an interim graduate assistant.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 13 · RECRUITING: THE BASICS
================================================================================================

13.1  Prospects
---------------
  About 2,500 prospects come out every cycle: high schoolers, about 110 junior-college
  players, and a handful of international kickers and punters. Every one has:
    * real ratings you can't see,
    * a public star rating (1-5) that is national opinion — about one in six is a full star
      off, which is where busts and steals come from,
    * three hidden priorities, most important first,
    * a personality (Loyal, Front-runner, Showman, Homebody, Quiet, Family-first) that decides
      how he reacts to losing, to other schools, and how soft his commitment is,
    * a high school, and a senior season you can follow.
  Your recruiting class is next year's freshmen. Up to 25 per class; rosters are capped at 85
  scholarships by position.

13.2  What recruits want (the ten priorities)
---------------------------------------------
  Early playing time   your depth chart at his position (who's in front of him next fall)
  Develops pros        your staff's development rating for his position
  Winning now          the poll and your record
  Fits his game        your scheme and his position
  Academics            the school's academic rating
  Facilities           recruiting and training facilities, and the stadium
  Close to home        same state, same region, or far away
  Tradition            the program's history
  Campus life          the campus
  Trusts the staff     your relationship with him, built by contact
  Effort lands hardest when it lines up with what HE cares about. Selling facilities to a kid
  who wants the ball on Saturday barely registers.

13.3  Hours and actions
-----------------------
  Your staff gets a pool of hours each week: about 46 plus your staff's recruiting rating,
  cut by about a third during the regular season (Saturdays take the staff's time), plus a
  recruiting practice week (+8), plus hours your AD pays for. The Machine perk adds 15%.
  No hours in the preseason week.

    Evaluate film              2h   information only: narrows his range, reveals priorities
    Call and text              1h   a little interest and trust
    Extend an offer            3h   required before almost anything else; lifts the ceiling
    Send the position coach    4h   solid interest, some scouting
    Host a campus visit        6h   he comes to a home game — the result matters
    In-home visit              8h   the biggest single pitch (family-first kids love it)
    Push for a commitment      4h   closing, when you're leading
    Official visit             4h   booked for a home game (see 14.2)

  Rules: interest is 0-100 per school; gains shrink as it climbs; it decays every week you go
  quiet; nobody gets past 45 without an offer; the third pitch to the same kid in a week
  lands much softer than the first. Evaluating buys information and no interest at all —
  that trade-off is the heart of recruiting.

13.4  The screens
-----------------
  RECRUITING HUB (tab 4 -> [R]): class status, position needs, top of your board, the wire,
  and every tool: [F] finder, [G] suggested for you, [1] your board, [2] your class,
  [3] national rankings, [4] news, [5] final class rankings, [6] budget and NIL,
  [Q] standing orders, [V] official visits, [W] war room, [H] hit or bust, [C] summer camp
  (preseason), [L] preferred walk-ons (from Week 8).

  FINDER ([F]): search the whole country. Filters: position, stars, state, region, status
  (open, soft, committed to you), national rank, your projection, interest in you, needs only,
  your region, hide your board, name. Sorts: national rank, stars, your projection, his
  interest, closest to campus, suggested fit. [A3] or [A1,4,7] adds to your board. In the
  HOME column, JC means junior college and INT international. The suggested list explains
  itself with tags: NEED, IN-STATE, REGION, SCHEME FIT, HARD SELL, INTERESTED, SLEEPER,
  REACH, COMMITTED.

  BOARD ([1]): up to 40 targets you chose; nobody adds to it for you. Columns: rank, player,
  stars, projection, interest, standing, offer, your NIL, and the leader. Marks: H hard
  commit, S soft, E signed early, Q in your standing orders, OV official visit booked.
  Keys: [#] card, [Q#] queue an order, [$#] NIL offer, [D#] drop, [S] sort.

  RECRUIT CARD: his stars, home, size, high school, senior season, pipeline ties, the
  crystal ball, official visits used; your scouting range; what he wants (??? until you
  learn it); the race (the top five schools and their interest); NIL; his position room next
  fall; and the last few things that happened in his recruitment. Keys: [1]-[7] actions,
  [Q] queue an order, [V] official visit, [N] NIL, [M] promise, [W] walk-on invite,
  [T] add/remove from board.

13.5  Standing
--------------
  Where you stand: LEADING, IN GOOD SHAPE, IN THE MIX, ON THE FRINGE, LONG SHOT, or NO CONTACT
  (he hasn't heard from you). Early in a cycle, when nobody has much of a hold, being in front
  of a thin field still reads as leading.

13.6  Commitments, decommitments, signing
-----------------------------------------
  A recruit commits when a school with an offer leads him by enough and his interest is high
  enough; better prospects take longer. At most two commitments a week during the season
  (three after). Commitments are hard or soft (Chapter 14.5). Losing seasons shake commits
  loose; rivals keep calling. The early signing period and National Signing Day finish the
  cycle. Players who picked a school that matched their priorities develop faster; bad fits
  stall.

13.7  NIL for recruits
----------------------
  Offer a scholarship first, then put money on top ([N] on his card or [$#] on the board).
  Money makes you louder in his ear every week — how loud depends on how much money matters
  to him, how the offer compares to his market (about $900K a year for a five-star, $300K
  for a four-star, $70K for a three-star, more at big-budget schools), and whether someone
  else is offering more. Money alone never gets a commitment. Open offers are held against
  your budget until he picks; a signee's deal is paid every year he's on the roster.

13.8  Class rankings
--------------------
  Classes are ranked nationally the way the services do it: the top of a class carries it,
  depth still counts. The final rankings show after every signing day.

  BENDING THE RULES. Two options in the recruiting hub carry real risk:
    [O] Overtime   ten extra recruiting hours this week (once a week). About a 6% chance the first
                   time that someone notices, rising 3% with each use in a season; caught means a
                   recruiting-violation case. Uncaught uses can leave a trace that surfaces later.
    [T] Back channel  contact a player on another roster before he's in the portal: through his
                   trainer (small push, ~6% risk), his high-school coach (~13%), or a direct call with
                   an NIL number (big push, ~26%). Restless backups move the most. He becomes likelier
                   to enter the portal and to choose you; caught means a tampering case, and if he
                   transfers to you the CAB gets another look.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 14 · RECRUITING: STANDING ORDERS, VISITS, PITCHES AND THE REST
================================================================================================

14.1  Standing orders (hub -> [Q], or [Q] on a card, or [Q#] on your board)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Any action on any recruit can be a standing order:
    once · every week · every other week · every week for N weeks · every week until he
    commits (to anyone).

  ALL ORDERS SIT IN ONE QUEUE, IN THE ORDER YOU CHOOSE. The same recruit can be in it as many
  times as you like: "call Johnny, call Steven, call Johnny again, call Richard, in-home
  Johnny".

  WHEN IT RUNS. At the end of each week, after your game, the queue spends whatever hours you
  didn't spend by hand, from the top down. An order that doesn't fit in the hours left is
  CUT for that week — and a cheaper order further down can still run. So the top of the
  queue is who you protect; the bottom is who gets dropped when hours run short.

  THE QUEUE SCREEN shows, before the week ends, what will happen: every order marked
  "runs", "cut — out of hours", or why it's waiting (needs an offer first, off week, ran
  this week, done). A red line marks where the hours run out. It counts an offer higher in
  the queue, so "offer Johnny, then call Johnny" both run.

  Keys:
    [A]        add an order: pick a recruit, an action, how often, what to pitch (calls and
               visits), and where in the queue
    [M a b]    move order a to position b (M 5 1 puts the fifth order first)
    [T#]       send an order to the top
    [C#]       copy an order (the copy sits right below it)
    [E#]       change how often an order repeats
    [X#]       remove an order
    [R]        run the queue now (each order runs at most once a week)
    [O]        coordinator autopilot

  Orders finish on their own: a one-time order after it runs, N-week orders when N runs
  out, "until he commits" orders when he commits, and anything for a recruit who has signed.

  Calls and visits in the queue sell the best thing your staff knows he wants (or the topic
  you picked when you set the order).

  COORDINATOR AUTOPILOT ([O] on the queue screen): your OC can work the offensive side of
  your board (QB RB WR TE OL K P) and your DC the defensive side (DL LB CB S) on their own —
  offers, film, calls, position-coach visits, closing — after your queue runs, up to the hours
  you give each of them. They never touch a recruit your queue worked that week.

  THE WEEKLY REPORT. The Recruiting Office mails you every week: how many orders ran and how
  many hours they used, which were cut, what the coordinators did, official visits,
  commitments, rating changes, rival talk and bidding wars.

14.2  Official visits ([V] on a card, or hub -> [V])
----------------------------------------------------
  Every recruit gets FIVE official visits, one per school. You book his visit for one of your
  remaining home games (4 hours); he has to hold your offer and be at least a little
  interested, and he can't be visiting somewhere else that weekend.

  The Saturday decides it:
    * the result — a win, a close win, who you beat, a ranked opponent, the rivalry; a
      blowout loss is a disaster, and beating an FCS team impresses nobody,
    * the crowd — how full, how loud, a night game under the lights,
    * the stadium — its recruiting appeal and especially the Recruiting Lounge,
    * what he cares about — a kid who wants to win weighs the result more; a kid who wants
      facilities weighs the building more; campus life counts for a campus kid.

  Every visit writes a report: A HOME RUN, A GOOD WEEKEND, FINE, FLAT or A BAD WEEKEND, with
  what he saw ("90,000 packed in for a night win over No. 12 Bayou State. He was on the field for the
  postgame.") and where you stood before and after. The visits screen lists what's coming up
  and how the ones that happened went. CPU staffs book visits too, and five-stars' visits
  make the news.

  Tip: book your best targets for your biggest home games — ranked opponents, the rival,
  night games — and they see your program at its loudest.

14.3  Pitches
-------------
  Calls, position-coach visits and in-home visits are conversations: when you do one by hand,
  you pick what you sell him on. The list shows what you know he wants first ("he told you
  this matters"), then your strongest cases, each marked your strong suit / a solid case /
  a thin case / a weak spot.
    * Hit one of his three priorities and it lands — harder the more he cares, and harder
      still if your case is strong. Now you know that priority.
    * Miss and he checks his phone: the pitch lands at about 60%.
  Pitching is a second way to learn priorities besides film. Enter lets your staff sell the
  best thing they know.

14.4  The living trail
----------------------
  SENIOR SEASONS: every high school recruit plays a season; his card shows his stats and his
  team's record through the current week. JUCO players show their sophomore season.
  RATINGS UPDATES: in Weeks 5, 9 and 13 the services re-rank the country. Players they had
  wrong move a star toward the truth. Risers and fallers make the wire; the weekly report
  tells you about the ones on your board. National ranks re-sort.
  SUMMER CAMP (hub -> [C], preseason only, once a year): invite up to 12 kids from your
  board. They work out in front of your staff: two levels of scouting, some trust and a
  little interest. The camp report says who looked better than his rating, who played to it,
  and who didn't look like it up close. Far-away four- and five-stars without much interest
  in you may pass.
  CAMP STANDOUTS: every summer a few kids everybody underrated tear up the camp circuit and
  gain a star.

14.5  Commitments: hard, soft and silent
----------------------------------------
  HARD commitments hold; SOFT ones keep listening and decommit and flip far more often. Loyal
  kids and big leads make hard commits; front-runners, showmen and narrow leads make soft
  ones. Your class and your board show which is which.
  SILENT commitments: some kids (quiet, homebody, family-first types) commit without telling
  anyone. To every school but his, he still looks open — you may be chasing a kid who is
  already gone. He announces it a few weeks later.

14.6  The crystal ball
----------------------
  For uncommitted recruits, the experts predict where he'll land and how sure they are
  ("Georgia 72%"). It's on his card, in the war room and on the wire, and "trending" news
  follows the swings among the four- and five-stars. It reads the race; it can be wrong.

14.7  The early signing period and National Signing Day
-------------------------------------------------------
  EARLY SIGNING (NP First Round week, mid-December): most hard commits sign; they're locked
  in and can no longer flip. Soft commits and close races often wait. The Recruiting Office
  tells you how many of your commits signed and who is still out there.
  NATIONAL SIGNING DAY (the offseason): the rest decide. Some soft commits FLIP to the school
  that kept after them. Over-recruited positions cost someone his spot (and his high school
  remembers). The undecided pick from the schools that offered, weighted by interest.
  You can watch it LIVE: your board first, then the country's top uncommitted kids — hats on
  the table, one kid at a time. [W] watches it live; Enter shows just the results.

14.8  Rivals fight back
-----------------------
  * NEGATIVE RECRUITING: rival staffs in a kid's top three use what they can: your hot seat
    ("you won't be the coach there in a year"), your losing record, a position room you've
    already stacked, the players he'd sit behind. It costs you interest, and it shows in his
    story on his card and in your weekly report.
  * NIL BIDDING WARS: from Week 11, when you're paying a four- or five-star and a rival is
    close, the rival's collective may raise its offer past yours. You'll hear about it.
  * THE CROWDED ROOM: "Why do you already have three quarterbacks committed?" Once a class
    fills a position's need, every pitch to more players at that position lands softer (about
    70%; half once you're past the need).

14.9  Pipelines
---------------
  Every recruit has a high school. Sign kids from a school and it becomes a pipeline: some
  ties, strong ties, a pipeline. Pitches to its players land harder (up to about 40% harder),
  and your staff gets free intel on them (their range narrows without film). Break a promise
  to one of its kids, or cut one at signing day, and that school goes cold. Ties fade a
  little every year. The recruit card shows your ties; the war room lists your pipelines.

14.10  More ways to find players
--------------------------------
  JUCO TRANSFERS (JC): older and better right now, less upside, and they want to play now.
  They arrive as juniors with two years left.
  INTERNATIONAL KICKERS AND PUNTERS (INT): big legs from ProKick Australia, rugby and Pro League
  academies.
  PREFERRED WALK-ONS (hub -> [L] from Week 8, or [W] on a one- or two-star's card): no
  scholarship — a roster spot and a chance. Invite up to six; they join after signing day if
  nobody else signs them. Walk-ons are cut first when a room is over the limit, but your
  preferred walk-ons are protected like scholarship players. One who becomes a real player
  (roughly a starter) earns a scholarship.

14.11  The war room (hub -> [W])
--------------------------------
  Your own big board, in your order. Each line: your tier (T1-T3), the player, his
  projection, where you stand, his commitment (and whether it's hard, soft or early), the
  crystal ball, whether he's booked an official visit, and how many standing orders he's
  in. Above it: your class needs by position and your CLASS GOAL (a top-10 class, a top-25
  class, three four-stars, every need filled, or the best class in your conference) with
  whether you're on track. Below it: your pipelines. Keys: [#] his card, [M a b] move,
  [T#] cycle his tier, [G] set the class goal, [H] hit or bust.

14.12  Hit or bust (hub -> [H])
-------------------------------
  Every class you've signed, graded on what the players became: HIT (playing like his stars
  said, or better), STEAL (a three-star or less who became a real player), BUST (two years in
  and nowhere close), plus developing, contributor, gone. Hit rates by star rating, your best
  steals, your biggest misses, and a line for each class.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 15 · THE TRANSFER PORTAL
================================================================================================

  The portal opens every offseason, between class advancement and the arrival of the
  freshman class. Players enter for real reasons: buried on the depth chart, a bad fit since
  signing day, no development, a losing program, a coach who left, low morale, a refused NIL
  raise. Starters playing well on winning teams don't go anywhere.

  The window runs in three rounds with standards dropping each time. About 88% of the portal
  finds a home; the rest are out of the sport. The market prices immediate help: being better
  than the man starting now is worth more than a rating, and quarterbacks move the market.

  YOUR WINDOW (Coach Career): the portal pauses after everyone enters and before anyone
  lands. You get two weeks' worth of recruiting hours to contact entrants (learn their
  interest), pitch them, host official visits, and offer roster spots (up to 8 — a player can
  only choose you if you offered). Put NIL on top of a roster offer ([N]); open offers are
  held against your budget. You can also try to talk your own players out of leaving (a raise
  helps). Your staff doesn't bid on its own, so the players you land are the ones you went
  after. A results screen shows who you landed and who got away.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 16 · MONEY: BUDGETS, CONTRACTS, NIL AND NEGOTIATING
================================================================================================

16.1  The budget (tab 3 -> [$])
-------------------------------
  Every program has an annual football budget set by its size and league: about $50M at the
  biggest SCC and Continental programs, $20-35M across the rest of the Power leagues, $4-12M in
  the Group of Five. It pays, in order: operations (20% off the top), the head coach, both
  coordinators, any buyouts, facility projects and stadium payments — and whatever is left is
  the player NIL pool. That pool pays the NIL on your roster, and what's left after that is
  what you can offer recruits.

  The budget moves once a year with results: winning seasons, league titles, playoff runs and
  titles grow it; winning above your weight grows it; a rising brand grows it; three straight
  losing seasons and coach after coach fired shrink it. It can reach about twice where it
  started or fall to 60%. Booster windfalls and realignment money move it too.

  The budget screen shows this season's spending, next class's NIL room (the budget left,
  returning players, commits, open offers, available, and a suggested class spend), the top
  deals, buyouts, the payroll, and every program side by side.

16.2  Contracts
---------------
  Every coach and coordinator signs for a salary and a number of years, written the AD's way:
  a yearly raise (1-5%), a buyout guarantee, maybe an offset clause (fire him and his next
  job's pay comes off what he's owed), a release clause (leave early and your new school pays
  your old one), performance bonuses (bowl, league title, playoff, national title), and maybe
  a rollover (every 8-win season adds a year). Booster-Driven ADs back up the truck; Budget
  Hawks write short, cheap deals with offsets; Win-Now ADs load up on bonuses.

16.3  Buyouts and release fees
------------------------------
  Fire a coach with years left and the school keeps paying part of his salary every year until
  the deal would have ended (less his new salary, with an offset). Hiring a sitting coach means
  paying his release fee to his old school.

16.4  Negotiating (Coach Career)
--------------------------------
  Every job offer and extension shows the whole contract and what it leaves for NIL. You can
  ask for 10% or 20% more money, more years, a bigger buyout guarantee, a smaller release
  clause, no offset, or less base for bigger bonuses. Every ask can make the AD walk away (a
  pulled job offer is gone; pulled extension talks mean you play out your deal). ADs have
  short, normal or long patience; harder asks are riskier; the risk climbs with every counter.
  And he remembers: whatever you squeeze out of him, your seat starts that much warmer. (Taking
  less base for bigger bonuses cools it.)

16.5  NIL on your roster
------------------------
  Every signee's NIL deal is paid every year he's on the roster. After the title game,
  underpaid starters ask for raises — and mercenaries add "or I'm in the portal." Pay,
  counter, or refuse.

  FINDING THE MONEY (Coach Career)
  --------------------------------
  Whenever something costs more than you have free — a coordinator's salary, a position
  coach, NIL for a recruit or a transfer, a raise to keep a player, a facilities project, a
  stadium fund deposit — the game opens FIND THE MONEY instead of just saying no. It shows
  what you need, what's free, the gap, and next season's books, then lets you:
    [1] Players          ask one to take 25% less (once a year each; a no hurts his mood),
                         or release him (his whole deal comes off; the locker room notices)
    [2] Coordinators     ask for a 15% pay cut (a no makes him likelier to leave), or let
                         him go (the screen shows what you'd still owe and the net saving)
    [3] Position coaches a 10% pay cut, or let him go (an interim GA takes the room cheaply)
    [4] Buyouts          ask a fired coach to spread next season's check over three years
                         (10% more in total)
    [5] Your salary      take a cut for the rest of your deal (your AD notices)
    [6] Facilities       cancel next season's project or a stadium fund deposit
    [7] Recruiting       pull NIL offers you've made to unsigned recruits
  NEGOTIATING. [N#] on a player or a coordinator sits you down with him instead of just
  asking for a cut. Every offer shows how it's likely to land (very likely ... a long shot);
  each kind can be tried once a year per person, and a near miss brings a counteroffer.
    Players       pay it back (20/35/50% less this year, all of it back next year +15%), or a
                  role promise for a backup (he'll start / play real snaps next fall — graded
                  at season's end like a recruiting promise)
    Coordinators  an extension (+2 years at a 3% raise for 10% or 20% less now — his buyout
                  grows), pay it back (back next year +12%), or the assistant head coach title
                  for 10% less (climbers love it)
    You           defer part of your salary (back next year +10%)
  Money promised for later sits on that season's books as "deferred pay"; deferred salary
  and NIL go back to normal the year after. [N1]/[N2] in the staff room opens the same table.
  Once the money's there you go straight back to finish what you were doing; [Enter] gives
  up instead. The budget screen ([$]) has [R] to open it any time.

================================================================================================
 CHAPTER 17 · FACILITIES, THE STADIUM, ATTENDANCE AND THE TOUGHEST PLACES TO PLAY
================================================================================================

17.1  Facilities (tab 3 -> [T] -> [F])
--------------------------------------
  RECRUITING FACILITIES (1-10): every recruiting pitch lands harder (0.94x at 1 to 1.12x at
  10), and it's most of what a recruit means by "facilities".
  TRAINING FACILITIES (1-10): players develop faster every offseason (0.94x to 1.08x).
  THE STADIUM: built from parts (below); its 1-10 grade is what the parts add up to.
  A recruiting or training upgrade is a one-time purchase out of next season's NIL pool, one
  project a season; each level costs more ($0.3M for level 2 up to $9M for level 10). Every
  offseason each can slip a level (2% plus 0.8% per level). The Groundbreaker perk takes 15%
  off; Blueprint stops slipping.

17.2  The stadium, part by part ([F] -> [S])
--------------------------------------------
  Nineteen parts, each with tiers:
    SEATING         Lower Bowl (+6,000 seats a tier), End Zone Stands (up to an enclosed bowl,
                    +4,000), Upper Deck (home side, then both sides, then wraparound, +5,500),
                    Student Section (+1,500), Club Seats (+1,200), Luxury Suites (20 a tier)
    ATMOSPHERE      Roof Canopy (also keeps the rain off the fans), Video Board, Sound
                    System, Stadium Lights (standard, then an LED light show), Team Entrance
    FAN EXPERIENCE  Concourses (open-air to climate-controlled), Fan Plaza (parking lots to a
                    Gameday Village), Hall of Fame, Press Box
    FOOTBALL        Home Locker Room, Visitors' Locker Room (spartan, or pink), Recruiting
                    Lounge, Playing Surface (worn turf to heated hybrid grass; grass gets
                    sloppy in the rain, heated hybrid grass drains and melts snow)

  What the parts do:
    * SEATS: capacity, and $45 a game for every new seat you actually sell.
    * NOISE (1-10): steep decks, enclosed ends, a student section, a canopy, a big sound
      system. With the crowd, that's the home-field edge and how hard momentum swings.
    * MONEY: club seats, suites, concessions and video-board ads, sold to what the market can
      pay. Stadium revenue is added to next season's budget.
    * RECRUITING APPEAL (0-100): what a recruit sees on a visit — a quarter of his
      "facilities" read, every pitch a little harder, official visits (the lounge above all).
    * IN THE GAME: a good home locker room is worth a little at home; a spartan or pink
      visitors' room takes a little from the other team; worn turf means more home injuries,
      good grass fewer.
    * FANS: concourses, the fan plaza and the hall of fame grow the fan base.

  Building: one stadium project at a time; big ones take two or three seasons (a seating
  project closes a section while it's built). Many parts need others first: an upper deck
  needs a horseshoe lower bowl, suites need a press box, a canopy needs an upper deck.
  Screens, speakers, turf, lights, the locker room and the lounge age and can slip a tier.

  Paying: the STADIUM FUND pays first. Boosters give every offseason (more at a big, winning,
  sold-out program), and a conference title, playoff run or national title starts a capital
  campaign. The rest is paid from the NIL pool as it's built, or with a 6- or 10-season bond
  the fund pays back (+15% / +30%; if the fund runs dry, the NIL pool covers the payment).
  [D] moves NIL money into the fund.

  THE FAN BASE is the crowd a full-interest Saturday brings. It grows slowly with prestige,
  winning, sellouts and a good game-day experience. Build seats faster than fans and the place
  plays half-empty: quieter, and no ticket money. A small school can build an 80,000-seat bowl,
  but it takes decades, and a program good enough to fill it.

17.3  Attendance
----------------
  Every home game draws a real crowd: the fan base, how this season is going (a program that
  expects to win and doesn't sees empty seats), last season, the poll, the opponent, a night
  kickoff (more under an LED show), and how comfortable the stadium is. Rivalry games,
  postseason games and big home openers sell out — if the fan base can fill the place.

17.4  The toughest places to play (tab 5 -> [S])
------------------------------------------------
  A live ranking of every FBS home field: the building's noise (20 points), the crowd's size
  and fill (20), the home record (35 — this season counts double, plus the last three),
  ranked visitors sent home beaten (12), the home streak (8), and tradition (5). Pick a team
  for its breakdown; [Y] shows every finished season's final list. The booth mentions it.

 CHAPTER 18 · JOB SECURITY: YOUR AD, GOALS, THE HOT SEAT, FIRINGS AND OFFERS
================================================================================================

18.1  Your AD
-------------
  Twelve styles judge coaches their own way: Patient, Win-Now, Big-Game Hunter, Conference
  First, Analytics, Booster-Driven, Turnaround (wants more wins every year), Recruiting-First
  (grades the classes), Traditionalist (the rival and the home record), Budget Hawk (hates
  buyouts either way), Brand Builder (ranked and on TV) and Fan Pulse (talk radio). A new AD
  can arrive any winter with new priorities and no loyalty.

18.2  Goals
-----------
  Each AD sets three program goals from 28 kinds — a winning season, beating the rival,
  winning more than last year, a bowl, a conference title game, going unbeaten at home,
  winning one-score games, scoring or defense targets, a top recruiting class, and more.
  Each has a window ("at least once in 3 years"); you have to hit it inside its window. The AD
  starts judging goals after a grace period that depends on his style.

18.3  The seat
--------------
  The hot seat runs 0-100: Ice cold, Stable, Warm, Hot, Scorching. It moves every Saturday:
  how the year is going against what the AD expected, plus the games people remember — the
  rival, a loss to an FCS or Group of Five team, an upset, a blowout, a losing streak, a big
  win. Your press answers and inbox replies move it too. The verdict comes after the season.

  Expectations are game by game: every game is scored for how likely your roster was to win
  it, there. You're judged on wins above or below that. Early in your tenure it's the roster
  you inherited; by year four it's also what the program's stature says it should win.

18.4  AD trust
--------------
  Trust (0-100) is built by keeping your word, answers he likes and goals met; spent by broken
  promises, asking for money, missed goals and silence. It changes how hard results hit your
  seat and how much money he finds for you. A new AD starts over.

18.5  Getting fired, extended, or poached
-----------------------------------------
  * Firing: two hot years or one disaster puts a coach on the edge. Then the AD checks the
    buyout and whether anyone better is available. A winning season usually buys a year.
    A coach at the end of his deal with a warm seat can simply not be renewed. You're never
    fired mid-season; CPU coaches can be (an interim finishes the year).
  * Extensions: after a big season, when the deal gets short, or to keep bigger programs away.
    If your seat is hot when your deal ends, it isn't renewed.
  * Offers: win, and bigger jobs call every offseason — openings where you're a top candidate,
    and sometimes a big program willing to push its own coach out to get you. Take one, ask
    for more, or stay.
  * Fired? You go into the pool. Smaller programs love a big name who got fired somewhere
    bigger.

 CHAPTER 19 · THE COACHING CAROUSEL AND THE STAFF CAROUSEL
================================================================================================

  Every offseason, in order:
    1. Every season is graded; seats are set.
    2. Coaches age; some retire (or leave for the Pro League).
    3. The AD market: presidents judge their ADs; ADs retire, get fired, get hired away.
    4. Firings (and contracts left to run out).
    5. Expiring contracts are redone. Your contract talks happen here.
    6. Splash-hire ADs push out a lukewarm coach for a star.
    7. Your phone rings first; then every opening is filled, biggest programs first.
    8. Coaches who stayed get early extensions.
    9. The staff carousel: new head coaches clean house, coordinators whose units flopped get
       fired, openings fill. Your staff review happens here.

  Twelve career personalities decide how coaches handle offers: Climber, Loyalist, Mercenary,
  Builder, Homebody, Journeyman, Alma Mater Guy, Blue-Blood Chaser, Fixer, Survivor,
  Short-Timer and Lifer. Hiring: bigger jobs interview more people, nobody interviews a coach
  the budget can't pay, a thin roster wants a recruiter, Power jobs want Power experience,
  nobody rehires the coach it just fired. A coach who just took a job or just signed to stay
  can't be hired away again the same winter.

  Tab 6 -> [K] coaches and the carousel: every head coach, coordinators, rising coordinators,
  the carousel report and news.

  HIRING ON A RESUME. Nobody hiring a coach sees his ratings or his ceiling. An AD sees what
  the world sees: results against what the roster should have done, the level he did it at,
  rings and playoff trips, his recruiting classes, a coordinator's units, his record before
  this world began, and how he interviews. A sharp AD reads that paper in context; a weak one
  reads last season's record, the rings and the interview. Sometimes the hot name flops and
  the quiet one turns out to be great. Coach pages show his Résumé grade (how the profession
  sees his record, not a rating). In Athletic Director mode your search shows the same thing:
  RES (résumé) and CLS (his classes). Coordinators are hired on their résumés too, and pay
  follows the résumé.

  ATHLETIC DIRECTORS. Every AD has a style (how he judges a coach), two character traits, an
  age, and a competence nobody sees: it shows in how well he reads a résumé and, over the
  years, in how his hires turn out. Traits: Numbers Person, Headline Reader, Alumni First, Old
  Network, Penny Pincher, Big Spender, Loyal to a Fault, Itchy Trigger, Youth Movement, Old
  School, Coordinator Scout, Regional Ties, Gambler, Steady Hand, Fundraiser and Campus
  Politician. Presidents judge ADs on football against expectations and on the coaches they
  hired. Bad ones get fired and join the pool of available ADs; a good AD at a smaller school
  gets hired away (and his old job opens); old ADs retire; deputies, small-school ADs,
  outsiders and former coaches enter the pool every year. Tab 6 -> [K] -> [A] Athletic
  Directors: every AD with his style, traits and reputation, the available pool, every move,
  and a profile with his earlier jobs and how each of his hires turned out. A new AD has no
  loyalty to the coach he inherited.

  AGE. Every coach has a hidden age when the game starts to pass him by. Most start slipping
  in their early sixties; about one in six is fading by his mid-fifties; about one in ten is
  still great into his seventies (and keeps coaching longer).

  THE HEAD COACH SEARCH. Every opening's search is tracked: the shortlist the AD worked
  from, who said no and why, whose school matched to keep him, who got the job. The carousel
  phase of the offseason shows the tracker; [T] on the offers screen opens it too. When schools
  call you, [I#] interviews with that AD: three questions, and what he wants to hear depends
  on who he is. Three good answers: more money and an extra year. None: a smaller offer, or
  it's pulled.

  THE STAFF CAROUSEL
  ------------------
  After the head coaching carousel, every assistant opening in the country is filled one at a
  time from early December to late January, biggest programs first, in a dated news feed:
    * openings come from firings, new head coaches cleaning house, retirements, assistants who
      walk out after their coordinator is fired, and every hire that empties a chair somewhere
      else — the dominoes ("↳ opens the OC job at ...")
    * each coordinator search interviews three: proven coordinators, fired head coaches, and
      position coaches ready for a first coordinator job (★ in the feed — a career event)
    * candidates say no: happy where they are, a counteroffer, waiting for a bigger job,
      staying with their coordinator
    * a new coordinator brings his guys and bumps whoever had the room
  In Coach Career the carousel is its own phase on the offseason calendar. Your openings come
  up in turn (your own position coaches are on the coordinator list — promote from within),
  and sometimes your pick turns you down. When a program wants one of your coaches, the phone
  rings: give him your blessing (a promotion grows your coaching tree) or try to keep him with
  a raise. The phase ends with what it meant for you: your staff, your tree, and the first
  coordinator jobs around the country. [V#] on the calendar replays the whole feed.

 CHAPTER 20 · RANKINGS, THE PLAYOFF, BOWLS, AWARDS AND THE DRAFT
================================================================================================

20.1  The media poll (tab 5 -> [T])
--------------------------------
  A preseason poll from prestige, roster strength and last year, re-voted every week like
  real voters: ballots are sticky (at most 7 spots up or 14 down in a week), losses cost more
  than wins, who you lost to matters most, unbeatens are protected, idle teams barely move,
  and strength of schedule plus a Group of Five discount keep mid-majors in their lane.

20.2  The NP rankings and the committee (tab 5 -> [P] / [C])
-------------------------------------------------------------
  Twelve members (former coaches, ADs, players, an administrator, a president, a writer) on
  staggered three-year terms. Each reads the same résumé — record, strength of schedule,
  quality wins, bad losses, capped margin, conference titles, the eye test, recent form —
  and weighs it his own way. Each hands in a Top 25; a team's number is its average ballot
  spot blended 70/30 with the media poll. First release after Week 9, final after the
  conference title games. Open any team or member to see the ballots.

20.3  The postseason
--------------------
  Conference Championships (Week 14): the top two in each league. The NP: 12 teams — the five
  highest-ranked conference champions plus seven at-large — first round on campus,
  quarterfinals and semifinals at the Six Classics bowls in the real rotation, the title
  game in January. 35 more bowls fill the rest, best teams to the best bowls.

20.4  The Golden Helmet and awards
----------------------------
  The Golden Helmet race is a weekly straw poll (production weighted by position, scaled by team
  success). Awards season: the Golden Helmet, the best player at every position, Freshman and Coach
  of the Year, All-Americans.

20.5  The Pro League Draft
-------------------
  7 rounds, 224 picks. Underclassmen decide whether to declare from where they project; some
  gamble and go undrafted. On your team, you sit down with each one projected in the first
  five rounds. Programs that produce picks earn a prestige bonus that fades over three drafts
  (and the Pro League Pipeline perk turns them into recruiting pull).

20.6  National champions (tab 5 -> [N])
---------------------------------------
  The real BCS and Playoff eras (1998-2025), plus every title won in your world.

20.7  Strength of schedule (tab 5 -> [O], tab 2 -> [O], [S] on any team schedule)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  How good a team's opponents are, measured the way the committee reads it: each opponent's
  roster plus 25 x his winning percentage (an FCS school counts as a weak roster at .300).
  Early in the season a record is blended with last season's, so September isn't judged on
  one or two games. Every FBS team is ranked 1 (hardest) to 138 three ways:
    FULL      every game on the schedule, played or not (postseason games once they're set)
    PLAYED    the games so far
    AHEAD     the games still to play (the "who's got the easier road" list in November)
  Words: brutal (top 15), tough (16-40), average (41-85), soft (86-115), very soft.
  Opponents' record is what a team's FBS opponents did in their other games.

  Where it shows: every team's schedule (under the record), the team page, the dashboard's
  schedule panel, and the window's sidebar. The rankings screen sorts by any of the three
  ([F] [P] [A]), narrows to one conference ([C]), opens a team's schedule ([T#], by rank),
  and keeps every finished season's final list ([Y]).

  In Coach Career you see ranks and words, never the index: it's built from ratings your
  staff can't see. The other modes show the index too.

 CHAPTER 21 · THE LIVING WORLD: RIVALRIES, REALIGNMENT, PRESTIGE, CLASSICS, MEDIA
================================================================================================

  PROGRAM HISTORY AND COACH CAREERS. [Y] on any team page opens the program's whole history,
  a page at a time ([N] next, [P] previous, [O] oldest or newest first): every season with its
  record, conference record, league, final rank, postseason, head coach and recruiting class
  (★ marks a conference title); [C] the coaching eras with each man's record, ranked seasons,
  playoff trips, conference titles and national titles; [T] the trophy case (titles, bowl wins,
  Golden Helmet winners, award winners, All-Americans, draft classes); [V] the all-time series
  against every opponent (rivals first, with the streak and the last meeting). On a coach's
  page, [F] opens his full career the same way: every season, every job's totals, his
  coordinator years, every move and why, and his career before this world began. Type
  "Texas history", "program history" or "coach career" anywhere to jump there.

  RIVALRY GAMES. In a rivalry game the underdog plays above itself and the day is less
  predictable for both sides: upsets come about ten points more often than in an ordinary game
  with the same gap. The Rivalry Trophies screen (Media Center) lists every trophy game and
  every named rivalry, with the holder or the last winner.

  SEASON FORM (team momentum). Every result moves a team's run of form: wins up, losses down,
  upsets and statement wins more, blowout losses more. It fades about a third each week, so it
  tracks the last three or four games, and every season starts level. A red-hot team carries
  up to about 1.2 rating points into Saturday (a field goal or so on the scoreboard), a
  reeling one loses about a point; a hot team facing a much weaker one gets half of it (the
  trap game), and a reeling team in a rivalry game or against a ranked team only half the
  drag. It tips close games and never makes anyone unbeatable or winless. Team pages show it
  ("Season form: Rolling ▲, last 5: W W L W W"), and so does the poll in the story kit.

21.1  Rivalries and trophies (team page -> [V])
-----------------------------------------------
  Every game goes in a series book. 48 trophies are tracked (the Bronze Heifer, the
  Northwoods Saw, the Magnolia Cup, the Copper Kettle, the Canyon State Cup...). The booth knows
  the series, the streak, who has the trophy, and the last meeting. Rivalry games always sell
  out (if they can) and swing recruits.

21.2  Conference realignment
----------------------------
  From the winter after 2026, schools change leagues for real reasons: TV value (brand,
  winning, market, crowd), media payouts, exit fees, distance, rivals. Moves are announced one
  winter and happen the season after next; split rivalries become protected non-conference
  games. Budgets move with the money. In AD mode, an invitation is your call.

21.3  Prestige, brand and legacy
--------------------------------
  Prestige has two halves: LEGACY (the decades; drifts slowly toward the last dozen years) and
  BRAND (the last five seasons; moves fast). Recruits, transfers and staffs read brand. Every
  winter, rarely: a BOOSTER WINDFALL (a big budget jump and a rebuilt facility or stadium
  upgrades) or CAB SANCTIONS (a postseason ban, two years of recruiting restrictions, a
  brand hit).

21.4  Coaches
-------------
  Great coaches are rare; most have a few points of room to grow, about one in six has real
  upside, about one in twenty-five is a genius. ADs hire on reputation, not the true number,
  and every coach has a hidden fit with each school.

21.5  Instant Classics (Media Center -> [14], team page -> [I])
---------------------------------------------------------------
  Every game is judged when it ends: huge upsets, overtime marathons, walk-offs, comebacks,
  shootouts, record performances, big-stakes thrillers. The best are marked LEGENDARY. About
  twenty a season.

21.6  The Media Center (tab 7 -> [M])
-------------------------------------
  The Wire (headlines), Players of the Week, Stat Leaders, Team Rankings, Hot Seat Watch, the
  Injury Report, a Playoff Projection, Award Races, the Campus Countdown Desk, the Awards Archive, every
  Pro League Draft, Rivalry Trophies, Conference Realignment, Instant Classics, and the Parity Report
  (who's risen and fallen since 2026, windfalls and sanctions).

 CHAPTER 22 · ATHLETIC DIRECTOR MODE
================================================================================================

  You run the program; the coach recruits and calls plays. You decide who the coach is.

  PRESEASON: make the Statement (a rebuild, show progress, bowl or bust, contend, championship
  or bust). It sets the win total the fans judge you against: big promises buy goodwill in
  August and cost double in December. Set the coach's three goals and price the tickets.

  EVERY WEEK: the fans react to Saturday against what you promised (the rival counts double,
  blowouts sting), the boosters follow the fans, and about half the weeks something lands on
  your desk — a naming-rights deal, a broken weight room, a coach who wants recruiting staff,
  a "fire him" banner plane, a new video board, a donor, a TV network, the NIL collective.
  Each choice moves money, fans, boosters or the coach.

  THE AD OFFICE ([C] from any tab): facilities and the stadium, upkeep (deferred, standard or
  premium — how often buildings slip), ticket prices (value, standard, premium), the budget,
  the coach's goals, extending him (you write salary, years, buyout, bonuses, a per-goal bonus
  and a rollover), and firing him mid-season.

  SEASON'S END: the report card (the promise, the goals, crowds and money), then the board
  grades you. Then your call on the coach: keep, extend, let his deal run out, or fire him (the
  buyout is on you). Your call is tied to that coach: if he's already gone by the time the
  carousel runs, nobody else is fired in his place. Lose the board and you're fired — take a
  smaller job or walk away. Keep it high three years and bigger schools call.

  THE SEARCH: if the job is open you see a real list — coaches out of work, sitting head
  coaches (with the release fee), coordinators — each with an asking price and his agent's
  read on his interest. Write the offer. Other schools can come after your coach: match or let
  him walk.

    Game lines    spread and total at -110, moneyline, first-half spread and total, team totals
    Props         featured games only; a prop on a player who doesn't play is void
    Parlays       2-12 legs; one side (spread, moneyline or first-half spread) and one total

    The Boost     one two-team moneyline parlay a week, boosted about 30% ($50 max; [X]).
    Futures       straight bets only.

  HOW THE BOOK SETS ITS NUMBERS
    The rating gap between two teams becomes a margin on a curve fitted to how games really
    end (a moderate favorite wins by more than a straight line says; mismatches flatten out),
    so favorites cover about half the time at every size of spread. On top of it: what the
    season has taught the book (a lot in September, less by November; half carries into next
    year) and about a point of public money on the big brands. Home field is the same
    everywhere (about 3.5 points) — the book doesn't know which buildings are really loud, or
    which coaches win on Saturday. Moneylines come from the same number (about 18 points of
    noise either way) with the vig on both sides; totals come from the offenses, the
    defenses, that week's weather and the league's scoring climate. Futures come from playing
    out the rest of the season a few hundred times with the book's numbers.
    Lines close at kickoff — you can bet a game from its matchup card until it starts
    ([B] Bet this game). A bet locks the number you took.

  SETTLEMENT
    Bets are graded the moment a game is final; a parlay loses the moment one leg does. After
    every week you play, a results screen shows what paid; bets settled while you simmed are
    waiting in the hub. Win totals pay after Week 13, conference titles on championship
    Saturday, make-the-playoff on Selection Day, the Golden Helmet and the national title when the
    season ends.

23.3  The regulars and the standings
------------------------------------
  Twelve regulars bet at The Window every week — made-up former players and coaches, each

  week's lines settle, so their tickets are up all week.
    THE FILM ROOM      his own number: the market's read without the public money, plus how
                       loud the building really is and who's coaching. Bets 1.5+ points off.
    THE MODEL          the same kind of number, on moneylines, sized by (quarter) Kelly.
    CHALK              ranked favorites laying points, favorite moneyline parlays.
    THE DOG POUND      underdogs of a touchdown or more; a small longshot moneyline.
    THE HOMER          his old school every week; their conference title and win total.
    FADE THE PUBLIC    the other side of a favored blue blood.
    UNDER THE WEATHER  unders in bad weather and between good defenses.
    POINTS PLEASE      overs when tempo offenses meet, or a bad defense plays.
    THE LOTTERY        a long parlay, a teaser and the Boost every week.
    HOT HAND           teams covering the number (and fading teams that aren't).
    THE BOOSTER        long-shot futures and one big ticket on the game of the week.
    YARDS GUY          player props: yardage overs and anytime touchdowns.
  Some of these are real edges and some are superstitions; the standings will tell you which.
  They start with your bankroll. Go broke and they re-up with $1,000 (it's counted).

  D STANDINGS: everyone, you included, by this season's winnings ([K] all-time), with bankroll,

 CHAPTER 24 · THE OFFSEASON, STEP BY STEP
================================================================================================

  After the title game, [A] advances to the offseason. Before it starts, the game walks you
  through NIL raise demands and underclassmen weighing the draft. In AD mode, your report
  card and your call on the coach come first.

  THE OFFSEASON HUB (Coach Career)
  ---------------------------------
  The offseason now uses the same rhythm as the regular season: 17 numbered weeks, one home
  hub, and [A] to open that week's agenda. There are no game simulations, so these weeks move
  much faster than fall Saturdays, but the calendar itself is just as long.

  The hub is your information base. It keeps the next several offseason weeks on the left and
  your live program state on the right: recruiting rank, transfer-class rank, overall incoming
  class rank, roster count, available budget, staff status and unread mail. Below that, the
  NATIONAL BOARD changes with the time of year (coaching-carousel news, portal/recruiting
  movement, incoming-class results, then the preseason poll), while LAST SEASON keeps the
  finished record and important team-stat ranks in view.

  Press [A] for WEEK AGENDA. The agenda separates the primary decision from warnings and
  deadlines. Required weeks cannot be advanced until the primary decision is handled; quiet
  weeks can be advanced immediately after you review the information. [I] opens the inbox, [R]
  the roster, [$] the budget, [S] the staff room and [V] saves.

    W01 JAN  Season Fallout          firings, retirements, head-coach searches
    W02 JAN  Your Staff              keep/fire/hire decisions
    W03 JAN  Staff Carousel          assistant interviews, poaching and dominoes
    W04 JAN  Portal Window I Opens   your winter portal work
    W05 JAN  Portal Market           transfer board / class movement
    W06 JAN  Portal Deadline         final winter-window information
    W07 FEB  Signing Push            final recruiting board before NSD
    W08 FEB  National Signing Day    portal results and the class signing live
    W09 FEB  Program Reset           awards and the roster report
    W10 MAR  Winter Development      review offseason growth before spring
    W11 MAR  Spring Ball I           plan + three practice sessions per player
    W12 APR  Spring Ball II          second practice block, battles, position trials
    W13 APR  A-Day                   Blue vs White film and spring depth movement
    W14 MAY  Portal Window II Opens  post-spring entries and recruiting
    W15 MAY  Portal II Deadline      final transfer calls + Draft report
    W16 JUN  Summer Workouts         talent/room outlook, conditioning, camp prep
    W17 JUL  Media Days              poll, conference prediction, watchlists, honors

  The three-phase offseason rebuild is now complete. The hub/calendar owns time and information;
  recruiting, both portal windows, coaching searches and spring football advance as persistent
  processes underneath it. That means saving in the middle of January, spring ball or Media Days
  preserves the offseason exactly where you left it.

  SPRING BALL. Weeks 11-12 are two real practice blocks, each with three practice samples for every
  player. Choose Fundamentals, Competition, Young Players, Physicality, Chemistry or Passing Game.
  Spring work can produce a little real development, but practice STOCK is separate: it changes what
  your staff believes about a player without secretly turning every good practice into OVR. The best
  and worst spring trends stay visible in the offseason hub. Spring injuries can happen too. Before
  A-Day you can still use POSITION TRIALS to try up to two players somewhere new.

  A-DAY is the third spring evaluation point. Blue vs White generates actual game production; that film
  moves spring stock, can nudge morale and rebuilds the staff's spring depth order before Portal Window II.
  A player who ends spring buried or whose stock fell is therefore more vulnerable to the post-spring
  portal. A-Day remains a recruiting event for in-state and targeted prospects.

  SPRING FOCUS. Spring practice lists the close battles (the last starter against the first
  man behind him, as your staff sees it). Each one you pick gets your staff's full attention,
  and for the whole season that follows their read at those positions is far sharper: the
  practice report and the depth chart they recommend are close to the truth there. You get a
  verdict right away ("clear winner", "has the edge", "by a hair"), and your staff rebuilds
  the depth chart with it.

  WHAT RUNS UNDERNEATH, IN ORDER
    1. The season is archived (every box score kept).
    2. The coaching carousel (Chapter 19), then the staff carousel and your staff phase.
    3. Records roll into history; stats into careers.
    4. Brand and legacy move; booster windfalls; vacated wins come off the books; walk-ons who
       became players earn scholarships; high-school pipelines fade a little. (Before the carousel:
       finals, every team's APR, and the winter stretch in which CAB cases move: Chapter 29.)
    5. Awards season and the Pro League Draft.
    6. Seniors graduate; everyone develops (two-thirds of a year's growth); redshirts are
       decided; classes move up a year.
    7. NIL raises are settled; the transfer portal runs (your window).
    8. NATIONAL SIGNING DAY: commits sign, some soft commits flip, the undecided choose.
    9. The freshman class arrives (JUCOs as juniors), your preferred walk-ons join, walk-ons
       fill the holes, rosters are trimmed to 85.
   10. Conference realignment takes effect; TV deals renew.
   11. A new year: new FCS rosters, a new schedule, a new recruiting class, a fresh
       preseason poll. Every staff builds its depth chart.
   Then the calendar picks up at Signing Day and runs through spring and summer.

  Other modes (AD, spectator) and simulated seasons skip the calendar: you see the carousel
  report, awards, the draft, the offseason report, realignment, the portal and signing day.

 CHAPTER 27 · STRATEGY AND TIPS
================================================================================================

  YOUR FIRST SEASONS
  * Read the goals and their windows. "Once in 4 years" goals are not this year's problem.
    The AD judges you against what your roster should win; a 6-6 season with a weak roster
    can be a good year.
  * Bank your salary into Coach Skill and your recruiting rating early. Recruiting hours and
    a sharp staff's eye pay back every week for the rest of your career.
  * Take the free respec when you change jobs if your tree doesn't fit the new program.

  RECRUITING
  * Evaluate before you pitch. Two hours of film tells you what a kid wants; every pitch after
    that lands harder. Or pitch what you think he wants and learn from his reaction.
  * Recruit where you are. In-state and in-region kids feel proximity; pipelines compound.
  * Offer early, close late. Nobody passes 45 interest without an offer, and interest decays
    when you go quiet — that's what standing orders are for.
  * Build the queue once: offers at the top, then "call every week until he commits" for your
    core targets, then in-homes for the ones you're close on. Put the must-gets at the top and
    the long shots at the bottom, so hours run out on the kids you care least about.
  * Book official visits for your biggest home games. A night win over a ranked rival in a
    full stadium is the best pitch in the game. Don't book your top target for an FCS game.
  * Watch the crowded room. Once you've filled a position's need, more players there are a
    hard sell — spend the hours on another position.
  * Soft commits aren't done. Keep calling them until they sign in December.
  * Walk-ons and JUCOs fill holes cheaply. JUCOs help now; walk-ons are lottery tickets.

  GAME DAY
  * Play the big moments until you know your team; coach every snap in the games that matter.
  * Practice focus is free points: game-plan heavy for big games, rest & recover when you're
    banged up, recruiting week in a bye or a cupcake week.
  * Halftime adjustments are real. Lean on the run with a lead; air it out when you're down.
  * Read the tablet. When the coordinator names a play, it's because of what they've shown in
    that down and distance. Max protect when the sacks pile up; two deep when they're over
    the top; spy a quarterback who's running on you.
  * Know your players before you yell at one. The position coach tells you who he is.
  * Friday's plan is worth the minute: a key the film likes is worth more than one it doesn't.
  * Set a default routine once (Big game is a fine start) and most weeks are one key. Save
    your own for the weeks you always handle the same way — a bye-week recruiting push, a
    rivalry week. THE WEEK'S WORK after each game shows which habits are paying.
  * The chart is a rule of thumb. In this game fourth-and-short converts often and two-point
    tries work about two times in three, so the numbers go for it more than the chart does —
    especially late, when the score and the clock matter more than field position.
  * Check the forecast. Snow, heavy rain or a gale: pick Weather prep, and if you're calling
    plays, run it and keep the ball short. With a strong wind at your back, take the deep
    shot and try the long field goal; into it, punt. A warm-weather team visiting the
    Continental in November is a real edge for the home side.

  THE PROGRAM
  * Stadium: build fans before seats. A student section, a sound system and a recruiting
    lounge are cheap and pay off in noise and recruits. Seats nobody buys are quiet seats.
  * Keep your word. Promises, press answers and inbox replies all build AD trust, and trust
    decides how hard a bad Saturday hits your seat.
  * Don't squeeze every contract. Whatever you take from the AD warms your seat.

 CHAPTER 29 · COMPLIANCE & INTEGRITY: RULES, INVESTIGATIONS, GRADES AND OFF-FIELD TROUBLE
================================================================================================

  Every program in the country lives with a quiet risk: somebody, somewhere, bends a rule. A
  booster's dealership leases cars for nothing. An assistant texts a recruit in a dead period.
  A collective pays a starter for appearances he never makes. A tutor writes a paper. Most of it
  never comes out. Some of it becomes the story of a decade. The CAB starts watching in 2026;
  the four years of world-building before that are clean.

  Where to find it: tab 3 TEAM, tab 6 COACH or tab 7 MEDIA -> [Y]; in AD mode, [Y] in your
  office. The Compliance Wire (headlines from every program) is also Media Center item 16.

29.1  How a case unfolds
------------------------
  HIDDEN       A violation happened and nobody outside knows. Each week it can be self-reported
               (a strong compliance office finds its own problems) or found: a whistleblower, a
               newspaper, a rival's tip. Angry players and a big name make it more likely.
               A hidden case nobody finds in four years quietly goes away.
  REPORTS      A story runs. Cooperate, say nothing, or attack it. Some stories die.
  INQUIRY      The enforcement staff is in the building. Cooperate fully, hire outside counsel,
               or give them the minimum.
  ALLEGATIONS  The Notice of Allegations (Level I and II). Accept and negotiate, contest, or
               announce penalties on yourself first.
  RULING       The Committee on Infractions decides. Level I and II can be appealed: about one
               appeal in three wins some relief, and a denial stings.

  Level III (minor) cases skip the allegations stage and end with a fine and a reprimand.
  Level II and I bring the real penalties.

29.2  What decides the penalty
------------------------------
  Cooperation, self-reporting, outside counsel and penalties you announce yourself all shrink
  it. Stonewalling grows it. Contesting is a coin flip: win and the penalty drops by a third,
  lose and it grows. A cover-up (you knew and buried it) makes it 25% worse, and if it comes
  out it can move a Level II case up to Level I ("the school sat on it").

  Penalties, from lightest to heaviest: a fine (out of next year's NIL pool) · probation ·
  fewer recruiting hours · scholarship cuts (a smaller signing class; the recruiting screen
  shows the new cap) · players held out · booster and collective restrictions (a smaller NIL
  pool) · vacated wins (and titles) · a postseason ban · a head coach suspension for a Level I
  matter, in Coach Career: he misses games he should be coaching · heat on the seat. A ban and
  the recruiting cut are the same sanctions the rest of the game already reads.

29.3  How the trouble starts
----------------------------
  Risk depends on the athletic director's style (booster-driven and win-now programs bend more,
  patient and traditionalist ones less), how strong the compliance office is, the program's
  reputation for integrity, how big a program it is, and the shortcuts you agree to. A program
  on probation behaves. The compliance office screen shows your risk as a multiple of the
  average program's.

  In Coach Career, temptations reach your inbox: a booster who wants to "take care of" a
  recruit's family, an assistant who wants to text a kid in a dead period, a collective's
  "appearance" deal, a call to a rival's unhappy starter. Say no and you're clean. Say "I don't
  want to know" and you get what you wanted, plus a violation that is now out there, waiting.
  Nothing tells you when it comes out.

  Your own compliance director may also find something and ask: self-report it (credit for
  cooperation), bury it (a cover-up), or look into it first (a coin flip on which you end up
  with). An internal audit ([A] in the hub) finds what your office has missed and reports it for
  you, but it can't find what you decided to hide.

29.4  Academics
---------------
  Every player has a GPA, shown as words in Coach Career (Dean's list, solid, borderline, at
  risk, failing). It comes from the school's academics, the player's football IQ, and who he is
  (a Grinder or Film Junkie studies; a Diva or Coaster doesn't). Midterms (week 7) and finals
  (the offseason) move it. A player below 2.0 loses eligibility: mid-season for the rest of the
  year, after finals for the start of the next.

  Week 5 brings a note from your academic advisor if a player who matters is on the brink: tutors
  and study hall (costs recruiting hours), a professor who "finds" extra credit (fixes it, and
  leaves a violation behind), or sit him until he earns it.

  APR is a team score built from the school's academics, how many players are ineligible, how
  much you spend on academic support and how many incidents the team had. 930 is the line.
  One year below it is a warning and a hit to your reputation; a second, or a score under 905,
  is a postseason ban. Academic support (Lean, Standard, Elite) trades money for grades and APR.

29.5  Off the field
-------------------
  Arrests, DUIs, failed tests, conduct-code cases, a viral post, a bet on a game. Players with
  trouble traits are likelier. Other schools' athletic departments handle theirs, in their own
  style (win-now and booster programs shorten suspensions for starters, and repeat offenders
  get dismissed). On your team you choose: suspend him, handle it internally (he plays, and
  the story may come out), or dismiss him.

29.6  Reputation, and what it costs
-----------------------------------
  INTEGRITY is the program's reputation for running a clean shop. Investigations, rulings and
  incidents lower it; time and a clean file bring it back. It is worth up to about two points
  of recruiting pull either way. In Coach Career, the AD's patience and trust move with news
  of a violation, with rulings and with how you handle it. In AD mode the board and fans react,
  and CAB matters land on your desk before anything else that week.

  AD mode: the same decisions come as desk events; if you do nothing, the default is the
  cooperative one. You also set the compliance office budget and academic support level.

29.7  The screens
-----------------
  [1] Cases · [2] Academics (watch list) · [3] Compliance office (budget, risk) · [4] Sanctions
  and history · [5] League wire · [6] Under investigation (every open case, worst first,
  and who is sanctioned) · [7] APR board · [A] Internal audit · [B] back

  Spreadsheet export adds a Compliance tab: Compliance Cases, APR & Integrity and Academics.

  A note on the story: the wrongdoing is done by made-up boosters, staffers and players. AI
  head coaches are never accused of anything; the head coach answers for the program, so the
  seat heats up, as it would in real life. Your own coach can take the full penalties.

 CHAPTER 30 · THE RECORD BOOK AND THE HALL OF FAME

30.1  The record book (tab 5 RANKINGS -> [R], or a team page -> [E])
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Every record set from the first game of 2026 on, for the whole FBS and for every school.

  [G] Single game   passing, rushing, receiving, all-purpose, defense and kicking, including
                    the longest run, catch and field goal
  [S] Single season the finished seasons, with this year's leaders mixed in while it's going
  [R] Career        added up from every season, at one school or all of them; players still
                    playing climb the board week by week (marked "active")
  [T] Team: game    points, margin of victory, total, rushing and passing yards, combined points
  [Y] Team: season  wins, points, points per game, fewest allowed per game, point differential,
                    and the longest winning streaks (active streaks are listed too)
  [H] Coaches       wins, national titles, conference titles and seasons (games coached from 2026)

  [N] national · [M] your school · [O] any school · [+] top 3 or top 10.

  When somebody breaks a national record (and it's a truly big number), or a school record
  after the book has a season behind it, or passes the single-season mark mid-season, it makes
  the headlines. A player who transfers keeps one career: each school's book counts what he
  did there, the national book counts all of it.

30.2  The College Football Hall of Fame (tab 5 -> [F])
------------------------------------------------------
  WHO'S ELIGIBLE. A player two seasons after his last college game. A head coach with six or
  more seasons on the books (from 2026) once he's retired or been out of a head job for two
  seasons. The staff keeps a career file on every player who did something worth remembering,
  so a player's case survives graduation, the draft and the portal.

  THE CASE. Honors (the Golden Helmet, the position awards, All-America teams), production at his
  position against what an elite career looks like, national titles, where he was drafted,
  and records he holds. Linemen are judged on the tape and their honors. A coach: wins,
  winning percentage, national and conference titles, playoff trips.

  THE VOTE. Every offseason, after the draft, a ballot goes to a panel of 20 writers and former
  coaches ([M] shows them). Each has a lean: the numbers guy, the one who wants rings, the
  award voter, the line guy, the regional voter who favors his part of the country, and the
  old-school voter who keeps the Hall small. Each votes for up to 8 players and 2 coaches.
  75% gets you in. A class is at most 6 players and 2 coaches (highest share first), so a
  great candidate can miss in a crowded year. Under 5%, or 8 years on the ballot, and he's off
  for good. A couple of voters retire every year.

  [C] the latest class: each inductee's career, honors, share of the vote and a line from
  his speech · [A] every inductee, class by class · [V] every ballot: the full vote, and [#]
  on a name shows his case and who voted for him · [W] ballot watch: who's eligible next,
  and which active players are building a case · [P] program halls · [M] the panel.

30.3  Program halls, and your selections
----------------------------------------
  Every school has its own Hall of Fame, judged on what a player or coach did there (a coach
  needs four seasons at the school and a .600 record or a title). National inductees go into
  their school's hall too. Other schools' committees induct up to two a year.

  YOUR SCHOOL (Coach Career, AD mode): the committee brings you its nominees every offseason,
  strongest case first, with the ones it recommends pre-selected. Pick up to three ([#] to
  select, [D] to induct them). You can do it from the offseason report ([S]) or any time from
  the Hall of Fame hub ([S]). If you never get to it, the committee's picks go in when Week 1
  kicks off. Anyone you pass on can be nominated again next year.

  Team page -> [G] shows any school's hall.

 CHAPTER 31 · CUSTOM PROGRAMS: TEAM FILES AND THE TEAM BUILDER

31.1  Team files
----------------
  A team file is one program as a .json file in the custom_teams folder next to the game:
  everything the game keeps about it.

    Identity      school, nickname, abbreviation, short name, chant, conference, division,
                  home state, town, altitude
    Stadium       name, capacity, indoor or not, the level of every part of the building
    Ratings       offense, defense, coach, athletic director, facilities, academics,
                  tradition, campus (1-99)
    Money         recruiting / training / stadium facility levels (1-10), the athletic budget
    Head coach    name, age, skill, offense and defense schemes, aggression, personality,
                  alma mater, and the six development and recruiting ratings
    Coordinators  OC and DC: name, skill, scheme
    AD            name and style
    Flavor        primary rival, rivalry trophy, lines for the broadcast booth, tailgate dish
    Replaces      (optional) an existing program this one takes the place of
    Roster        (optional) every player: name, position, number, class and redshirt,
                  height, weight, home state, stars, potential, the six fundamentals,
                  proficiency by position, traits

  Anything left out is filled in; anything out of range is pulled back into range and you're
  told what changed. The easiest way to start: export any program (team page -> [J] -> [X])
  and edit the file. custom_teams/EXAMPLE_sacramento_valley.json shows every field.

31.2  In a new world
--------------------
  Main menu -> New game -> pick a mode -> [C] add custom programs first. [F] adds a team file,
  [N] builds one from scratch in the team builder, [#] edits or removes one, [G] builds the
  world. A file that replaces a program takes its place (and its conference, unless the file
  names one; rivals and trophies follow it); any other file joins its conference as a new
  program (Independent if its conference isn't in the game).

  Custom programs are built with the world, so they arrive with four years of recruiting
  history, rosters that match their ratings, and a coach with a career. A file with a roster
  keeps exactly that roster (walk-ons fill any position that's short).

31.3  In a save (team page -> [J])
----------------------------------
  [E] Edit this program: the team builder, on everything the program has. [A] applies it in
  place. Players you edit keep their stats, history and records; players you add join the
  roster; players you delete leave it. [X] Export to a team file. [L] Load a team file over
  this program (you choose whether to take the file's roster). [N] Build a new team file.

  Changing a program's name in a save carries its record book, Hall of Fame, rivalry series
  and coaches' records with it; old box scores keep the old name, because that's who played.
  A conference change takes full effect with next season's schedule. Your own program: you
  stay the head coach. Program ratings changed in a save move recruiting, prestige and money
  from now on; they don't re-grade the players already on the roster (edit the roster for that).

31.4  The team builder
----------------------
  [1] Identity · [2] Stadium · [3] Program ratings · [4] Facilities & money · [5] Head coach ·

 CHAPTER 33 · UNIVERSES: WHICH PROGRAMS, COACHES AND NAMES A WORLD USES
================================================================================================

33.1  What a universe is
------------------------
  A universe is everything a world is built from that has a name: the 138 FBS programs
  (school, nickname, abbreviation, stadium, chant), their head coaches, the 70 FCS
  opponents, the conferences, rivalry names and trophies, team lore, tailgate food, the
  bowls and their stadiums, the playoff rotation, the pregame show and its crew, the top
  award, and the history a world inherits.

  The game ships with its own universe: original programs, coaches and names, built on real
  states, towns and stadium sizes, with the same conferences, rivalries and schedule shape
  as the real sport. Its history starts in 2026.

33.2  Picking one (Settings -> [U])
-----------------------------------
  [#] picks the universe NEW worlds are built in. Universe files are the .json files in
  the universes/ folder next to the game; each one is listed with its name.

  A save always remembers its universe and switches back to it when it loads, whatever
  Settings says. Saves made before v29 were built from real-world data: they load with
  the real_2026 universe if universes/real_2026.json is there.

33.3  Making one
----------------
  [X] on the Universe screen writes the universe in use right now to a file you can edit
  (export the built-in one for a complete template). A file has a name, a one-line
  description, "tables" and "terms":

    tables   any of the game's tables by "module.NAME" (teams_data.TEAMS,
             coaches_data.HEAD_COACHES, rivalries.TROPHIES, postseason.BOWLS...). Leave one
             out and the built-in version is used. The full list is in universe.py.
    terms    display-only swaps: wherever the game prints the first words, you see the
             second (the award, the show, the playoff, the governing body, the pro league,
             the media poll). Saves and rules keep the built-in words.

  Rules that care about particular names (which conferences are the Power four, which
  independent is treated like one, who plays weeknights, the noon showcase rivalry) read
  them from world.py, which a universe can also set.

  Custom team files (chapter 31) work in any universe. A file's conference is matched
  through the universe's terms, so a file that says High Country joins the Mountain West
  in a universe that calls it that.

33.4  Sharing the game
----------------------
  The built-in universe is the one to share. Keep any universe file built from real teams,
  coaches or trademarks for your own copy.

OFFSEASON HUB — PHASE 2: THE OFFSEASON RACE
--------------------------------------------
  The offseason uses the same week-to-week rhythm as the regular season. Press [A] from the offseason hub to open
  that week's agenda; the hub itself is the information base for deadlines, national movement and program status.

  WINTER PORTAL (Weeks 4-6)
  * The portal is no longer settled all at once. Players enter with hidden decision timing: some move immediately,
    some wait for the market, and some take the process to the deadline.
  * Your contacts, visits, interest reads, offers and NIL work remain on the same portal board from week to week.
    Later weeks add follow-up recruiting time instead of resetting your work.
  * A verbal portal commitment is not always final. A small number of players can back off and reopen recruitment
    before the window closes. The deadline is real: once Week 6 advances, the winter market settles.

  RECRUITING / SIGNING DAY (Weeks 7-8)
  * High-school recruiting stays live until National Signing Day. The final offseason recruiting week runs another
    national recruiting tick, so late commits, decommitments and class-rank movement can still happen.
  * Week 8 is the Signing Day deadline. Commitments sign, unsigned players choose, late flips can happen, and roster
    capacity rules still apply. Recruiting, portal and overall incoming-class rankings are shown through the hub.

  POST-SPRING PORTAL (Weeks 14-15)
  * A second, smaller window opens after A-Day. Players who lost spring depth battles are more likely to enter,
    making spring evaluation and depth decisions matter immediately.
  * The second window resolves over two weeks and closes before summer/fall-camp preparation.

  COACHING NEWS / YOUR JOB MARKET
  * The opening offseason weeks reveal the coaching carousel as a continuing news cycle: firings, searches,
    interviews and hires remain visible on the national board while roster-building work is happening.
  * In a normal one-coach career, programs calling for you no longer force an instant carousel decision. Week 1
    shows the market and interview interest; Week 2 is the actual offer decision. An open job that is actively
    waiting on you is held out of the AI hiring pool until you accept or decline, and taking a job immediately
    switches the rest of the offseason to your new school.


OFFSEASON HUB — PHASE 3: SPRING & PRESEASON
--------------------------------------------
  SPRING BALL I / II (Weeks 11-12)
  * Every player receives three practice-session samples in each block. Stock-up / stock-down results affect staff
    evaluation and depth opinions; real development is tracked separately and is much smaller than winter growth.
  * Spring emphasis changes who gets the best work. Focused position battles sharpen the staff's read, while
    Physicality / Competition carry a little more injury risk. Spring evaluation fades after Week 1 as real game
    film replaces it.

  A-DAY (Week 13)
  * Blue vs White is real spring film, not only a scoreboard. Production changes spring stock and the staff depth
    order; strong or rough performances can move morale before the second portal window.
  * Portal Window II reacts to spring: buried players and players whose stock fell are more likely to look around.
    Once it opens, the hub shows winter and post-spring transfer-class ranks separately and folds both windows into
    the Overall Incoming Class rank.

  SUMMER / MEDIA DAYS (Weeks 16-17)
  * Summer shows national roster-talent rank and the strongest / weakest rooms entering fall camp.
  * Media Days shows the leading preseason teams, your conference finish projection, projected wins, national talent rank,
    position-room strengths and weaknesses, award watch-list players, and projected preseason All-America /
    All-Conference mentions. The completed offseason then hands directly to the normal Fall Camp hub.


CUSTOM PLAYER DATABASES
-----------------------
Before building a new world, choose [P] Choose custom player database. The game reads .json files from the `custom players` folder inside the game directory and shows them in a numbered in-game list. No operating-system file picker is used. Add or remove JSON files in that folder to change the available databases. Imported players replace generated roster slots rather than increasing roster size.


COMMISSIONER MODE (DISCORD LEAGUES)
-----------------------------------
  New Game -> [5] Commissioner. You run a league whose programs are coached by Discord members; you are the
  only one with the game. There is no coach of your own: the league runs from the COMMISSIONER DESK.

    [A]  advance one cycle (one week in season; the whole offseason is one cycle for now). Autosaves after.
    [T]  teams & owners: [C#] hand a program to a member (C12, or C then a school name), [R#] make it a CPU
         team again. A member coaches one program at a time.
    [I]  the Commissioner Inbox: decisions that need you (job offers, vacancies, new members and import
         problems arrive here as later builds switch them on).
    [D]  league directory: every program, its owner or OPEN (CPU). A text copy goes to the exports folder.
    [R] results  [S] standings  [P] polls  [V] save  [Q] main menu

    [O]  import order codes (from the desk, the teams screen or the inbox): put the Google Form's responses
         CSV (or a .txt of codes) in the game's `imports` folder. You read the report before anything applies.
    [M]  one team's orders by hand: a late code, or the orders as JSON
    [U]  roll back to a snapshot (taken before and after every import, and before every cycle)
    [W]  export the website   [K] new website password   [L] league title & links (see THE LEAGUE WEBSITE)
    [E]  members: every member, his program (or FIRED / FREE / KICKED) and his coach
    [N]  league setup: title and links, members from a CSV (imports folder), the first website.
         COMMISSIONER_GUIDE.txt (in the game folder) has the setup steps and the every-cycle checklist.

  JOINING: [T], then C and the program. Type the member's Discord name, then choose his coach:
    the sitting head coach; his own coach (a member who was fired keeps his coach); or a new coach from his
    Discord form (first and last name, age 30-60, background 1-6, offense and defense by number or name,
    fourth down 1-3, blitz 1-3: the same choices Coach Career offers). Any time, even mid-season: the
    sitting coach is let go (an interim goes back to his coordinator job). The member starts with the staff's
    board as standing orders and both coordinators on autopilot; his password is issued at the next export.
  FIRINGS: ADs fire members' coaches the way they fire anyone, mid-season included. The program goes CPU
    under an interim and the Commissioner Inbox says so. Hand it to someone else, and give the fired member an
    open program with "his own coach". CPU schools can't hire members' coaches away.
  THE COACHING MARKET (offseason cycle): members rank up to three jobs on the website (My team → Jobs) and say
    what to do with a contract offer (sign, turn down, ask me). Fired members lose their programs and keep their
    coaches. Open jobs go in prestige order; each AD offers the member who'd stand highest on his list if he wants
    him. A ranked job's offer is accepted; anything else is asked on the desk while the cycle runs (the default:
    a fired member accepts, others pass). Every move opens the old chair, and the walk repeats. CPU schools never
    hire a member's coach. Contract offers to members work the same way.
  STAFF: members keep a ranked list of up to 10 names per chair (OC, DC, eight position rooms) with their best
    package, and can let coaches go in the offseason. When a member's chair opens in the staff carousel his list
    is worked in order on Coach Career's rules (interest, package, counters within his max, his school's
    retention bid, the AD's funding); nobody says yes → the AD hires. Members' coordinators aren't fired by the AI.
  MONEY: facilities upgrades (recruiting, training) and the Find the Money asks (players' NIL, coordinators'
    and position coaches' pay, stretching buyouts) come in members' codes, with Coach Career's odds.
  SEASON END: after the title game members answer NIL raise demands and talk to draft-eligible juniors on the
    website. Unanswered: the staff decides, as for any program.
  THE OFFSEASON, NINE CYCLES: 1 coaching carousel and staffs · 2 awards, draft, winter (seniors graduate,
    everyone develops, NIL raises settled) · 3 Portal Window I opens · 4 portal market (members' retention talks
    and offers; the window closes) · 5 signing push (a last recruiting week) · 6 National Signing Day (classes
    arrive) · 7 roster week (members' position changes and cuts; rooms trimmed; the new year begins) · 8 spring
    ball and A-Day (each member's emphasis) · 9 Portal Window II and summer. Fall camp → Week 1 comes next.
    Offseason cycles don't count toward inactivity.
  INACTIVITY: five missed cycles in a row asks you, in the inbox, whether to kick the member (his program
    goes CPU); it asks again every cycle he stays missing.
  AFTER [A]: the website is written automatically (turn it off in [L], Export after [A]). Upload site/.

  ORDER CODES are one line of text per player per cycle, signed with that team's own key. A code that was
  edited, made for another team or a previous owner, another league, or another cycle is rejected with the reason.
  WHAT ORDERS CONTROL: recruiting (board, the standing-orders queue, pitches, coordinator autopilot, official
  visits, NIL, promises, walk-ons), the depth chart by position, the game plan (practice focus, the offensive and
  defensive keys, scripted openers) and who calls plays. They stand until a newer code replaces them.
  PLAYER TEAMS do no AI recruiting: their orders run it. Miss two cycles in a row and the staff also works a
  basic board until the member is back. Signing Day's safety nets still fill holes, as for a human in Coach Career.
  SWITCHED OFF in a Commissioner league: sanctions (cases, investigations, penalties, academic holds),
  rule-bending (recruiting overtime, the back channel), discipline (locker-room incidents and team-rules
  suspensions), player inboxes, press conferences, rivalry-week moments and skill points. Difficulty is
  Varsity. Injuries and bowl opt-outs still happen.
  RERUN-IDENTICAL: every cycle is seeded from the world and the cycle number, so loading a save and running the
  same cycle again gives exactly the same results.

 CHAPTER 34 · ONLINE: FRIENDS ON THE SAME WI-FI

 Window note: when the game asks for input, the full question and choices stay in the main scrollable game screen. The bottom bar is only where you type your answer, so long online/live prompts are not clipped.

  Title screen -> [7] Online. Friends on the same home network share one world. Each of you runs the game on
  your own computer and coaches a different program; one of you hosts, and the host's world is the official one.
  Everyone needs the same version of the game (and, for a custom universe, the same universe file).

  HOSTING: [H], then a new world or any save (a career's world becomes everyone's; that coach stays in it, run by
    the CPU unless someone picks his program). Type your name, pick your program, and read out the ADDRESS and
    CODE on the lobby screen. The first time, your computer may ask to let Python accept connections: allow it on
    private (home) networks. When everyone has picked, [S] starts the world.
  JOINING: [J], type the address and the 4-digit code, your name, then your program: type a school (or part of
    one, or a conference) and its number. Coach as the sitting head coach, or bring your own (the Coach Career
    questions). A program someone already picked is theirs. [W] waits for the host to start. The host cannot start
    while somebody in the room is still choosing a program. A returning player reuses the same seat when possible.
  YOUR WEEK is a Coach Career week. [A] runs fall camp (Week 0), your bowl week, and the week hub as always:
    film, inbox, practice, the presser, the game plan. When you leave the hub, your orders go to the host and
    you're READY. When everyone is, the week kicks off: Saturday plays on your own computer, the show first and
    your game your way (sim it, the big moments, every snap, or watch), everyone else's around it. Then the
    week's results, your Weekly Wrap and the Recap, from the host's world.
  WHY YOUR GAME COUNTS: you play your game once, on your computer. When you finish, your copy sends the final
    score, full box score, player game/season totals and game injuries to the host. The host loads that result into
    the shared world and does NOT replay or simulate your matchup a second time. Imported online box scores expose
    the same opponent/team helpers as a normal played game, so records, rivalries, Instant Classics and week-end
    bookkeeping can consume them normally. Games with no connected coach are still simulated by the host. If your
    copy tries to submit work from an older world revision, it refreshes from the host rather than waiting forever
    on work the host could not accept.
  WAITING: two short waits are the only new screens: for everyone to be ready, and for everyone to finish their
    games. Both move on by themselves. Ctrl+C (or 45 seconds) offers "not ready after all", and for the host
    "don't wait: play it now" (a missing coach's staff plays his game). Repeated Ready/Force presses cannot queue
    extra offseason calendar steps. If the host's background world work fails, the wait screen shows the error
    instead of looking frozen; the host can retry the step.
  TWO OF YOU MEET: neither takes the headset; both staffs call it from your game plans. Watch it or sim it.
  [M] sim weeks is off online: weeks move when everyone's ready. Formation/play packages,
    preseason redshirt protection, retention conversations, role promises and the morale changes
    they create are standing team state too: the client sends them with the rest of its orders,
    and the host applies them before the shared world advances.
  YOUR INBOX AND THE PODIUM work as in a career: the mail, your replies, the Thursday press conference and the
    postgame podium all happen on your copy, on the same screens, and land in the official world exactly as you
    answered them (the host runs the same conversation as you, with what you typed). Each player has his own
    inbox, hub and career log on the host. A coach who never opens his hub still gets his week's mail.
  THE OFFSEASON moves the same way as the season: everybody works the same calendar step, everybody readies,
    then the host advances the shared world. January job/staff decisions, all three winter-portal weeks, the final
    recruiting push and Signing Day, spring work, both post-spring portal weeks, summer and Media Days stay on the
    Coach Career screens. If two coaches accept the same open job, the first accepted call wins and the other coach
    sees the filled chair on the next snapshot. Signing-Day room checks and other questions that happen inside the
    host's world appear live on the affected player's computer. Large spring/summer blocks replay with a fixed RNG
    stream and a team-state fingerprint so a mismatch is recorded instead of passing silently.
  NOT YET: saving the host's world as a resumable online session. Your copy is never saved on your computer; your
    seat is kept in online.json, so you rejoin it with the address. Only join hosts you know: like a save file, a
    world can run code when it loads.
